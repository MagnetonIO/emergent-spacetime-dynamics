Introduction

Modular framework, not unified theory

Modern theoretical physics is replete with frameworks that aspire to unification. The programme to which this paper belongs makes no such claim. We instead pursue a modular approach: we identify a small number of mathematical layers, each of which is autonomous and self-standing, and we organise their interaction through explicit composition hooks. The hooks are formal: they are functor signatures, sheaf conditions, or operadic action squares whose existence is asserted in Law I and whose realisation is supplied by Laws II, III, and IV.

This paper is Law I. It establishes the categorical primitives that the other three laws will compose onto. Its claims are not physical in the empirical sense—no Hamiltonian, no scattering amplitude, no thermodynamic measurement appears as a primitive object. Its claims are structural: if we accept that physical processes are arrows in some category 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys}, and if we accept that informational descriptions live in some category 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}, then the relation between them must be a structure-preserving functor whose domain and codomain admit specific composition properties.

Why categorical language?

Three reasons motivate the choice. First, physical systems compose, both spatially (tensor products of subsystems) and temporally (sequential evolution); the algebra of these two compositions, together with their compatibility, is exactly what monoidal category theory was designed to formalise . Second, observables organise locally and assemble globally: this is the sheaf-theoretic structure familiar from gauge theory, and its higher-categorical refinement underlies modern formulations of quantum field theory . Third, the same algebraic data—a symmetric monoidal closed (or compact closed) category—governs quantum mechanics, computation, logic, and topology, as exhibited in the Rosetta Stone of Baez and Stay . Taking the Rosetta Stone seriously means that the matter–information correspondence is not a physical accident but a four-way translational symmetry built into the categorical substrate.

Composition hooks: where Laws II–IV plug in

A central methodological device of this paper is the explicit declaration of composition hooks. A hook is a typed slot: it specifies the categorical signature that a downstream construction must populate. We declare eight hooks:

  • 𝖧𝟣\mathsf{H1} (Symmetry-action hook): a category of symmetry data 𝒢\mathcal{G} acting on 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} via a functor ρ:B𝒢→Aut(𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔)\rho: B\mathcal{G} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{Phys}). Used by Law II to encode global symmetries of phases of matter.

  • 𝖧𝟤\mathsf{H2} (Hamiltonian hook): a designated subcategory 𝑯𝒂𝒎↪𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Ham} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Phys} closed under tensor product. Used by Laws II, III to define gapped Hamiltonians and adiabatic deformations.

  • 𝖧𝟥\mathsf{H3} (Floquet hook): a circle category BℤB\mathbb{Z} and an action Bℤ→Aut(𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔)B\mathbb{Z} \to \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbf{Phys}). Used by Law III to encode periodic drive.

  • 𝖧𝟦\mathsf{H4} (Sheaf hook): a Grothendieck site (𝒞,J)(\mathcal{C}, J) controlling locality of observables. Used by Law II to handle local order parameters and by Law IV for boundary CFT data.

  • 𝖧𝟧\mathsf{H5} (Operadic hook): an operad OO acting on a designated object of 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}. Used by Law IV for factorisation algebras.

  • 𝖧𝟨\mathsf{H6} (Dagger hook): the dagger structure †\dagger on 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} and its preservation by MM. Used by Law III for unitary drive operators.

  • 𝖧𝟩\mathsf{H7} (Compact-closure hook): existence of duals in 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} and 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}. Used by Law IV for traces, partial traces, and the Ryu–Takayanagi formula.

  • 𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H8} (Type-theoretic hook): an internal language ℒ(𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐)\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{Info}) obtained from a topos structure. Used by all subsequent laws for executable encoding.

We mark these hooks throughout the text. They are the contract between Law I and the remainder of the series.

Categorical Preliminaries

We fix conventions. Throughout, “category” means a locally small category in the sense of . We follow the conventions of  when the discussion turns to (∞,n)(\infty,n)-categories.

Categories, functors, natural transformations

Definition 1 (Category). A category 𝒞\mathcal{C} consists of:

  1. a class Ob(𝒞)\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}) of objects;

  2. for every pair A,B∈Ob(𝒞)A, B \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}), a set Hom𝒞(A,B)\mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(A,B) of morphisms;

  3. for every triple A,B,CA, B, C, a composition ∘:Hom𝒞(B,C)×Hom𝒞(A,B)→Hom𝒞(A,C)\circ : \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(B,C) \times \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(A,B) \to \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(A,C);

  4. for every AA, an identity idA∈Hom𝒞(A,A)\mathrm{id}_A \in \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(A,A);

satisfying associativity (h∘g)∘f=h∘(g∘f)(h \circ g) \circ f = h \circ (g \circ f) and unitality id∘f=f=f∘id\mathrm{id}\circ f = f = f \circ \mathrm{id}.

Definition 2 (Functor). A functor F:𝒞→𝒟F : \mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{D} assigns to each A∈Ob(𝒞)A \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}) an object F(A)∈Ob(𝒟)F(A) \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{D}) and to each morphism f:A→Bf : A \to B a morphism F(f):F(A)→F(B)F(f) : F(A) \to F(B), satisfying F(idA)=idF(A)F(\mathrm{id}_A) = \mathrm{id}_{F(A)} and F(g∘f)=F(g)∘F(f)F(g \circ f) = F(g) \circ F(f).

Definition 3 (Natural transformation). Given functors F,G:𝒞→𝒟F, G : \mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{D}, a natural transformation η:F⇒G\eta : F \Rightarrow G is a family of morphisms ηA:F(A)→G(A)\eta_A : F(A) \to G(A), indexed by A∈Ob(𝒞)A \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}), such that for every f:A→Bf : A \to B in 𝒞\mathcal{C} the square $$\begin{tikzcd} F(A) \arrow[r,"F(f)"] \arrow[d,"\eta_A"'] & F(B) \arrow[d,"\eta_B"] \\ G(A) \arrow[r,"G(f)"'] & G(B) \end{tikzcd}$$ commutes.

The slogan, due to Lawvere , is that mathematics is naturally organised by adjunctions, and the present paper takes this slogan as a working principle.

Monoidal categories

Definition 4 (Monoidal category). A monoidal category is a tuple (𝒞,⊗,I,α,λ,ρ)(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I, \alpha, \lambda, \rho) where 𝒞\mathcal{C} is a category, ⊗:𝒞×𝒞→𝒞\otimes : \mathcal{C}\times \mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{C} is a bifunctor, I∈Ob(𝒞)I \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}) is a distinguished unit, and α,λ,ρ\alpha, \lambda, \rho are natural isomorphisms αA,B,C:(A⊗B)⊗C→∼A⊗(B⊗C),λA:I⊗A→∼A,ρA:A⊗I→∼A,\alpha_{A,B,C} : (A \otimes B) \otimes C \xrightarrow{\sim} A \otimes (B \otimes C), \quad \lambda_A : I \otimes A \xrightarrow{\sim} A, \quad \rho_A : A \otimes I \xrightarrow{\sim} A, satisfying Mac Lane’s pentagon and triangle axioms .

Definition 5 (Symmetric monoidal category). A monoidal category (𝒞,⊗,I)(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, I) is symmetric if equipped with a natural isomorphism σA,B:A⊗B→∼B⊗A\sigma_{A,B} : A \otimes B \xrightarrow{\sim} B \otimes A such that σB,A∘σA,B=idA⊗B\sigma_{B,A} \circ \sigma_{A,B} = \mathrm{id}_{A \otimes B} and the symmetry hexagon commutes.

Theorem 6 (Mac Lane Coherence ). In any monoidal category, every diagram constructed from the structural isomorphisms α,λ,ρ\alpha, \lambda, \rho commutes. Equivalently, the free monoidal category on one generator is equivalent (as a monoidal category) to the discrete category whose objects are bracketed words modulo associativity and unit, and whose only morphisms are identities.

Proof sketch. Apply Mac Lane’s strictification: every monoidal category is monoidally equivalent to a strict monoidal category, in which the structural isomorphisms are identities. The pentagon and triangle axioms suffice to ensure the strictification is well-defined. ◻

6 licenses our subsequent suppression of associators and unitors when no confusion arises.

Compact closed and dagger structures

Definition 7 (Compact closed category). A symmetric monoidal category 𝒞\mathcal{C} is compact closed if every object AA has a dual A*A^* together with morphisms ηA:I→A*⊗A,εA:A⊗A*→I,\eta_A : I \to A^* \otimes A, \qquad \varepsilon_A : A \otimes A^* \to I, called unit and counit, satisfying the triangle (or “zig-zag”) identities: (idA⊗εA)∘(ηA⊗idA)=idA,(εA⊗idA*)∘(idA*⊗ηA)=idA*.(\mathrm{id}_A \otimes \varepsilon_A) \circ (\eta_A \otimes \mathrm{id}_A) = \mathrm{id}_A, \qquad (\varepsilon_A \otimes \mathrm{id}_{A^*}) \circ (\mathrm{id}_{A^*} \otimes \eta_A) = \mathrm{id}_{A^*}. Diagrammatically (string-diagram calculus, anticipated from 22), the first identity says that an AA-strand (oriented upward) which is bent through a cup (ηA\eta_A) and back through a cap (εA\varepsilon_A) “snakes” into a straight AA-strand: $$\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=tiny, row sep=tiny] {}\arrow[d, no head] & {} & {}\\ A \arrow[d, bend left=90, looseness=2, no head] & A^* \arrow[d, bend right=90, looseness=2, no head] & {}\\ {} & {} & A \arrow[d, no head]\\ {} & {} & {} \end{tikzcd} \;\;=\;\; \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=tiny, row sep=tiny] A \arrow[d, no head]\\ {}\\ A \end{tikzcd}$$ The second identity is the mirror image with AA and A*A^* exchanged.

Definition 8 (Dagger category). A dagger category is a category 𝒞\mathcal{C} equipped with a contravariant involutive identity-on-objects functor †:𝒞op→𝒞\dagger : \mathcal{C}^\mathrm{op}\to \mathcal{C} such that (f†)†=f(f^\dagger)^\dagger = f. A dagger compact closed category is a compact closed category whose dagger is compatible with the duals via εA†=σA*,A∘ηA\varepsilon_A^\dagger = \sigma_{A^*,A} \circ \eta_A .

Example 9 (𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb}). Finite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces with linear maps form a dagger compact closed symmetric monoidal category. The tensor is the Hilbert tensor product, the unit is ℂ\mathbb{C}, the dual of HH is the conjugate space H*H^*, η:ℂ→H*⊗H\eta : \mathbb{C} \to H^* \otimes H is 1↦∑ie‾i⊗ei1 \mapsto \sum_i \bar{e}_i \otimes e_i for any basis {ei}\{e_i\}, ε\varepsilon is the evaluation pairing, and †\dagger is the Hilbert-space adjoint .

The cobordism category

Definition 10 (𝑪𝒐𝒃n\mathbf{Cob}_n). The category 𝑪𝒐𝒃n\mathbf{Cob}_n has as objects closed oriented (n−1)(n-1)-manifolds. A morphism Σ0→Σ1\Sigma_0 \to \Sigma_1 is a diffeomorphism class of compact oriented nn-manifolds MM together with an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism ∂M≅Σ0¯⊔Σ1\partial M \cong \overline{\Sigma_0} \sqcup \Sigma_1. Composition is gluing along the boundary; the tensor is disjoint union; the unit is the empty (n−1)(n-1)-manifold.

𝑪𝒐𝒃n\mathbf{Cob}_n is symmetric monoidal and compact closed; the dual of Σ\Sigma is Σ¯\overline{\Sigma} (orientation reversal).

Definition 11 (TQFT ). An nn-dimensional TQFT is a symmetric monoidal functor Z:𝑪𝒐𝒃n→𝑽𝒆𝒄𝒕kZ : \mathbf{Cob}_n \to \mathbf{Vect}_k (or 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb}).

Theorem 12 (Cobordism Hypothesis ). Let 𝑩𝒐𝒓𝒅nfr\mathbf{Bord}_n^{\mathrm{fr}} denote the (∞,n)(\infty,n)-category of framed bordisms. For any symmetric monoidal (∞,n)(\infty,n)-category 𝒞\mathcal{C}, the evaluation-at-the-point functor Fun⊗(𝑩𝒐𝒓𝒅nfr,𝒞)→∼𝒞fd\mathrm{Fun}^{\otimes}(\mathbf{Bord}_n^{\mathrm{fr}}, \mathcal{C}) \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}} is an equivalence, where 𝒞fd\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}} is the ∞\infty-groupoid of fully dualisable objects of 𝒞\mathcal{C}.

We use the cobordism hypothesis as a representability principle: physical functors are determined by where they send the point, provided we work in a setting with enough duals.

The Matter–Information Functor

The central object of Law I is a structure-preserving functor M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info}. We now define its domain, codomain, and structural conditions.

The categories 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} and 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}

Definition 13 (𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys}). The category 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} is a symmetric monoidal dagger category whose objects are physical systems, abstractly characterised. Morphisms are processes: arbitrary linear, completely positive, or unitary maps depending on context. The tensor product is composition of independent systems; †\dagger encodes time-reversal/adjoint; II is the trivial system.

Definition 14 (𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}). The category 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info} is a symmetric monoidal dagger category whose objects are information-theoretic resources. Examples include: classical or quantum information types; presheaves on a poset of measurement contexts; or sections of a sheaf of state spaces. Morphisms are information-preserving (or information-monotone) processes.

Remark 15. We do not commit to a single concrete realisation of 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} or 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}. Different choices give different instantiations of Law I; what is universal is the type of the functor MM and the structural properties it must preserve.

Definition of MM

Definition 16 (Matter–information functor). A matter–information functor is a strong monoidal dagger functor M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info}. That is, MM is a functor equipped with natural isomorphisms μA,B:M(A)⊗M(B)→∼M(A⊗B),ι:I𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐→∼M(I𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔),\mu_{A,B} : M(A) \otimes M(B) \xrightarrow{\sim} M(A \otimes B), \qquad \iota : I_{\mathbf{Info}} \xrightarrow{\sim} M(I_{\mathbf{Phys}}), satisfying the standard monoidal coherence diagrams , and additionally M(f†)=M(f)†M(f^\dagger) = M(f)^\dagger for every morphism ff.

Composition Hook 17 (Symmetry-action hook 𝖧𝟣\mathsf{H1}). Given a group GG with delooping BGBG, a symmetry action on 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} is a strong monoidal functor ρ:BG→Aut⊗(𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔)\rho : BG \to \mathrm{Aut}_\otimes(\mathbf{Phys}). We do not require MM to be invertible. Instead, we say that MM transports ρ\rho to an action ρM:BG→Aut⊗(𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐)\rho_M : BG \to \mathrm{Aut}_\otimes(\mathbf{Info}) if for every g∈Gg \in G the diagram $$\begin{tikzcd} \mathbf{Phys}\arrow[r, "\rho(g)"] \arrow[d, "M"'] & \mathbf{Phys}\arrow[d, "M"] \\ \mathbf{Info}\arrow[r, "\rho_M(g)"'] & \mathbf{Info} \end{tikzcd}$$ commutes (strictly, or up to natural isomorphism in a 2-categorical setting). When MM is fully faithful, such a ρM\rho_M exists and is unique on the essential image of MM, by the universal property of the image factorisation. When MM is not fully faithful, the existence of a compatible transported action ρM\rho_M is no longer automatic: distinct g∈Gg \in G may act identically on the essential image of MM, or the chosen symmetry of 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} may fail to descend to a well-defined action on 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info} at all. Law II therefore imposes this transportability as a hypothesis: in the setting of Law II’s phase classifier, the chosen symmetry of 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} must transport cleanly through MM to a compatible action on 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}, and the existence of such a compatible ρM\rho_M is part of the definition of a symmetric phase.

This hook is what Law II uses to encode global symmetries.

Determination of MM on generators

The cobordism hypothesis allows us to characterise MM on a generating subcategory.

Theorem 18 (Generation theorem for MM). Suppose 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} is presented as a free symmetric monoidal category on a set of generating objects {Xi}i∈I\{X_i\}_{i \in I} and generating morphisms {fj:Aj→Bj}j∈J\{f_j : A_j \to B_j\}_{j \in J}, modulo a set of relations RR. Then a strong monoidal functor M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info} is uniquely determined by the assignments M(Xi)∈Ob(𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐),M(fj):M(Aj)→M(Bj),M(X_i) \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathbf{Info}), \qquad M(f_j) : M(A_j) \to M(B_j), provided the assignments respect the relations RR, in the sense that both sides of each relation map to equal morphisms in 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}.

Proof. We invoke the universal property of the free symmetric monoidal category on a signature with relations.

Step 1 (Existence). The free symmetric monoidal category F({Xi},{fj})\mathrm{F}(\{X_i\}, \{f_j\}) on the generating data, before quotienting by RR, is constructed by taking objects to be finite formal tensor expressions in the XiX_i (modulo the strict-monoidal coherence) and morphisms to be equivalence classes of formal expressions built from the fjf_j, identities, structural symmetries, and the operations ∘\circ and ⊗\otimes, modulo the bifunctoriality of ⊗\otimes and the symmetric monoidal coherence laws. The data of MM on generators extends uniquely to a strong monoidal functor on F({Xi},{fj})\mathrm{F}(\{X_i\}, \{f_j\}) by induction on the formal expression: tensor products are mapped via the coherence isomorphisms μ\mu, compositions are mapped by composition, identities by identities, and symmetries by the symmetries of 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}.

Step 2 (Descent through relations). The presentation 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔=F({Xi},{fj})/R\mathbf{Phys}= \mathrm{F}(\{X_i\}, \{f_j\}) / R identifies certain pairs of formal expressions. The hypothesis that “the assignments respect RR” means that for each relation r=(e1∼e2)∈Rr = (e_1 \sim e_2) \in R, the images M(e1)M(e_1) and M(e2)M(e_2) are equal in 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}. The functor M̃:F({Xi},{fj})→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\widetilde{M} : \mathrm{F}(\{X_i\}, \{f_j\}) \to \mathbf{Info} therefore descends through the quotient to a unique functor M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info}.

Step 3 (Uniqueness). Any strong monoidal functor M′:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M' : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info} agreeing with MM on the generators must, by the same induction, agree with MM on all formal expressions, hence on all morphisms. Uniqueness follows. ◻

Corollary 19 (MM on the cobordism category). If 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔=𝑩𝒐𝒓𝒅nfr\mathbf{Phys}= \mathbf{Bord}_n^{\mathrm{fr}}, then MM is determined by the image of the point under MM, provided this image is a fully dualisable object of 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}.

Proof. Combine 18 with 12. ◻

Born-rule type formula

Theorem 20 (Born rule from compact closure). Let M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info} be a dagger compact-preserving strong monoidal functor between dagger compact closed categories. For any state ψ:I→A\psi : I \to A in 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} and any effect m:A→Im : A \to I, the squared norm ∥m∘ψ∥2:=(m∘ψ)†∘(m∘ψ):I→I\|m \circ \psi\|^2 := (m \circ \psi)^\dagger \circ (m \circ \psi) : I \to I is preserved by MM in the sense that M(∥m∘ψ∥2)=∥M(m)∘M(ψ)∥2M(\|m \circ \psi\|^2) = \|M(m) \circ M(\psi)\|^2.

Proof. Since MM is a dagger functor, M((m∘ψ)†)=M(m∘ψ)†M((m \circ \psi)^\dagger) = M(m \circ \psi)^\dagger. Since MM is functorial, M(m∘ψ)=M(m)∘M(ψ)M(m \circ \psi) = M(m) \circ M(\psi). Composition then gives the claim: $$M(\|m \circ \psi\|^2) = M((m \circ \psi)^\dagger \circ (m \circ \psi)) = M(m \circ \psi)^\dagger \circ M(m \circ \psi) = \|M(m) \circ M(\psi)\|^2. \qedhere$$ ◻

In 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb}, End(I)=End(ℂ)=ℂ\mathrm{End}(I) = \mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}, and ∥m∘ψ∥2∈ℝ≥0\|m \circ \psi\|^2 \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} is the Born probability |⟨m∣ψ⟩|2|\langle m \mid \psi\rangle|^2. 20 says this number is invariant under MM.

Composition Hook 21 (Compact-closure hook 𝖧𝟩\mathsf{H7}). A compact-closed structure on 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} and 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}, preserved by MM, supplies the trace and partial trace operations used by Law IV’s holographic codes. Monoidal Structure of State Spaces

We now examine the monoidal structure in detail and exhibit the diagrammatic calculus we will rely on throughout the series.

String diagrams and coherence

Theorem 22 (Joyal–Street ). For free symmetric monoidal categories, the string diagram calculus is sound and complete: two morphisms are equal if and only if their string diagrams are isotopic as plane graphs (with crossings recording the symmetry).

We use string diagrams freely. Wires denote objects, boxes denote morphisms, vertical composition is sequential, horizontal juxtaposition is tensor, and crossings denote σ\sigma.

States, processes, effects

In a symmetric monoidal category, a state of AA is a morphism I→AI \to A, an effect on AA is a morphism A→IA \to I, and a process is a general morphism A→BA \to B. In a dagger category, the dagger of a state is an effect.

Proposition 23 (Process–state duality). In a compact closed category, there is a bijection Hom(A,B)≅Hom(I,A*⊗B)\mathrm{Hom}(A, B) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}(I, A^* \otimes B) natural in AA and BB, given by f↦(idA*⊗f)∘ηAf \mapsto (\mathrm{id}_{A^*} \otimes f) \circ \eta_A.

Proof. The inverse is g↦(εA⊗idB)∘σB⊗A*,A∘(g⊗idA)g \mapsto (\varepsilon_A \otimes \mathrm{id}_B) \circ \sigma_{B \otimes A^*, A} \circ (g \otimes \mathrm{id}_A). The triangle identities ensure both compositions are identities; naturality follows from the bifunctoriality of ⊗\otimes and naturality of η,ε\eta, \varepsilon. ◻

Tensor decomposition and entanglement

Definition 24 (Pure separable state). A state ψ:I→A⊗B\psi : I \to A \otimes B is pure separable if it factors as ψ=(ϕA⊗ϕB)∘λI−1\psi = (\phi_A \otimes \phi_B) \circ \lambda_I^{-1} for some states ϕA:I→A\phi_A : I \to A and ϕB:I→B\phi_B : I \to B.

Definition 25 (Pure entangled state). A state of A⊗BA \otimes B is pure entangled if it is not pure separable.

Proposition 26. The state ηA:I→A*⊗A\eta_A : I \to A^* \otimes A in a compact closed category is pure entangled whenever AA is not isomorphic to II.

Proof. Suppose ηA=(ϕA*⊗ϕA)∘λI−1\eta_A = (\phi_{A^*} \otimes \phi_A) \circ \lambda_I^{-1}. Worked diagrammatic identity: the snake equation

We illustrate the diagrammatic style with the snake equation, which is the diagrammatic content of the triangle identities of 7. Wires representing AA are drawn as upward-oriented strands; wires representing A*A^* are drawn downward-oriented. The unit ηA\eta_A is a cup opening upward (a ∪\cup-shape with A*A^* on the left and AA on the right); the counit εA\varepsilon_A is a cap closing downward.

The first triangle identity says that an AA-strand which goes “up, around the cap, down through the cup, and out the top” is equal to the straight AA-strand: (idA⊗εA)∘(ηA⊗idA)=idA.(\mathrm{id}_A \otimes \varepsilon_A) \circ (\eta_A \otimes \mathrm{id}_A) = \mathrm{id}_A. Diagrammatically, this is the celebrated S-shape that straightens out into a vertical line:

The second triangle identity is the mirror image with A*A^*. Both identities are required for the compact-closed structure: the first one ensures that an AA-strand can be straightened, and the second one ensures the same for an A*A^*-strand. Together they say: in a compact closed category, “slack can be pulled out of any wire,” regardless of orientation. The whole framework of 3—in particular 20 (Born rule) and the trace structure of 21—relies on both triangle identities being available simultaneously, so we list them together as a single structural axiom rather than treating them separately.

This is the foundational identity used by the categorical formulation of quantum teleportation : the teleportation protocol amounts to a diagrammatic snake straightening, in which the input wire of Alice’s qubit is bent through a Bell pair (a η\eta) and a Bell measurement (an ε\varepsilon), arriving at Bob with no further operation needed beyond a Pauli correction. Law IV will use the same identity to derive the Ryu–Takayanagi-style entanglement-area formula in the HaPPY code .

Definition 28 (Dagger Frobenius algebra). Adjunctions and quantisation

A central organising device of the framework is the notion of adjunction. We recall the definition for completeness.

Definition 30 (Adjunction). Given functors F:𝒞→𝒟F : \mathcal{C}\to \mathcal{D} and G:𝒟→𝒞G : \mathcal{D}\to \mathcal{C}, we say FF is left adjoint to GG (written F⊣GF \dashv G) if there is a bijection Hom𝒟(F(A),B)≅Hom𝒞(A,G(B))\mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{D}(F(A), B) \;\cong\; \mathrm{Hom}_\mathcal{C}(A, G(B)) natural in A∈𝒞A \in \mathcal{C} and B∈𝒟B \in \mathcal{D}.

  • The forgetful functor U:𝑯𝒂𝒎→𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔U : \mathbf{Ham} \to \mathbf{Phys} has a left adjoint Free:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑯𝒂𝒎\mathrm{Free} : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Ham} assigning the free Hamiltonian (zero-coupling).

  • The inclusion 𝑺𝒆𝒕↪𝑪𝒂𝒕\mathbf{Set}\hookrightarrow \mathbf{Cat} has a left adjoint (free category on a set) and a right adjoint (codiscrete category).

  • Quantisation/dequantisation are conjecturally a Quillen adjoint pair between suitable categories of classical and quantum systems .

Proof. Sheaves and Topoi for Local Physics

We now turn to the sheaf-theoretic refinement of the framework. The intuition is that physical observables are localised: a measurement is performed in a region of spacetime (or, more generally, a context). The collection of observables in each region forms a set or vector space, and these collections fit together in a structure-preserving way.

Sites, presheaves, sheaves

Definition 32 (Site). A site is a small category 𝒞\mathcal{C} together with a Grothendieck topology JJ, i.e. for each A∈Ob(𝒞)A \in \mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{C}) a collection J(A)J(A) of covering sieves, satisfying the standard axioms (stability, transitivity, identity) .

Definition 33 (Presheaf, sheaf). A presheaf on 𝒞\mathcal{C} is a functor F:𝒞op→𝑺𝒆𝒕F : \mathcal{C}^\mathrm{op}\to \mathbf{Set}. A sheaf for the topology JJ is a presheaf FF such that, for every covering sieve S∈J(A)S \in J(A), the natural map F(A)→lim(B→A)∈SF(B)F(A) \longrightarrow \lim_{(B \to A) \in S} F(B) is a bijection.

Composition Hook 34 (Sheaf hook 𝖧𝟦\mathsf{H4}). The site (𝒞,J)(\mathcal{C}, J) controlling locality is a hook supplied by the physical context. Examples used in subsequent laws: (a) for Law II, 𝒞\mathcal{C} is the poset of open subsets of a sample manifold and JJ is the open-cover topology; (b) for Law IV, 𝒞\mathcal{C} is a Boolean poset of measurement contexts and JJ is the canonical topology of overlap.

Topoi and internal logic

Definition 35 (Grothendieck topos). A Grothendieck topos is a category equivalent to the category 𝑺𝒉(𝒞,J)\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}, J) of sheaves on a small site.

Theorem 36 (Giraud ). A category ℰ\mathcal{E} is a Grothendieck topos if and only if it satisfies:

  1. all small colimits exist and are universal;

  2. coproducts are disjoint;

  3. equivalence relations are effective;

  4. there exists a set of generators.

Proposition 37. Every Grothendieck topos ℰ\mathcal{E} admits a subobject classifier Ω∈ℰ\Omega \in \mathcal{E}, characterised by the property that subobjects of any A∈ℰA \in \mathcal{E} correspond bijectively to morphisms A→ΩA \to \Omega. The internal language of ℰ\mathcal{E} is intuitionistic higher-order logic.

Composition Hook 38 (Type-theoretic hook 𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H8}). The internal language ℒ(𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐)\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{Info}) of 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}, when 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info} has topos structure, supplies an executable encoding language. Subsequent laws use ℒ(𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐)\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{Info}) to write down phase classifiers, Floquet operators, and quantum codes as type-theoretic terms.

Lifting sheaves through MM

Theorem 39 (Sheaf-lifting through MM, in the topos-theoretic regime). Hypotheses. Suppose that for the present application of the framework:

  1. 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} is equivalent to the Grothendieck topos 𝑺𝒉(𝒞𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔,J𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔)\mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}_\mathbf{Phys}, J_\mathbf{Phys}) of sheaves on a small site;

Conclusion. Then MM arises as the inverse-image part m*m^* of a geometric morphism of topoi, induced by a unique morphism of sites m:(𝒞𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐,J𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐)→(𝒞𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔,J𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔)m : (\mathcal{C}_\mathbf{Info}, J_\mathbf{Info}) \to (\mathcal{C}_\mathbf{Phys}, J_\mathbf{Phys}). Proof. The argument proceeds in three steps.

Step 1 (Geometric morphism from MM). A geometric morphism f:ℰ→ℱf : \mathcal{E}\to \mathcal{F} of Grothendieck topoi is by definition an adjunction f*⊣f*f^* \dashv f_* in which the left adjoint f*f^* preserves finite limits. Conversely, given a finite-limit-preserving cocontinuous functor M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info}, the special adjoint functor theorem (applicable because 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} is locally small with all small colimits) supplies a right adjoint MR:𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐→𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔M_R : \mathbf{Info}\to \mathbf{Phys}, and the pair (M,MR)=(m*,m*)(M, M_R) = (m^*, m_*) is a geometric morphism 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐→𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Info}\to \mathbf{Phys}.

Step 2 (Site morphism inducing m*m^*). By the Comparison Lemma , a geometric morphism 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐→𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Info}\to \mathbf{Phys} between sheaf topoi on small sites is determined by its restriction to the (full subcategories of) representable sheaves. The Yoneda embedding y𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔:𝒞𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔↪𝑺𝒉(𝒞𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔,J𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔)y_\mathbf{Phys}: \mathcal{C}_\mathbf{Phys}\hookrightarrow \mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{C}_\mathbf{Phys}, J_\mathbf{Phys}) is fully faithful, and similarly for 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}. Pulling m*m^* back along y𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔y_\mathbf{Phys} and using the universal property of sheafification gives a unique functor m:𝒞𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐→𝒞𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔m : \mathcal{C}_\mathbf{Info}\to \mathcal{C}_\mathbf{Phys} such that m*(y𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔(c))=a(y𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐(m¯(c)))m^* (y_\mathbf{Phys}(c)) = a(y_\mathbf{Info}(\overline{m}(c))) for some lift m¯\overline{m}, where aa is sheafification. This mm is the site morphism in the statement, and the direction is forced: the inverse-image part of a geometric morphism between sheaf topoi corresponds at the site level to a functor running in the opposite direction.

Step 3 (Uniqueness). Any other site morphism m′m' inducing the same m*m^* would give the same restriction to representables (by Yoneda), hence agree with mm. Uniqueness follows. ◻

39 is the formal statement that local-observable structure is preserved by MM. The geometric-morphism formulation makes explicit that MM behaves like the topos-theoretic “pullback of observables along a generalised continuous map between informational and physical sites.”

Operadic Composition Laws

Operads control multi-input operations and are essential for the algebraic description of observables in QFT.

Operads and their algebras

Definition 40 (Operad in 𝑺𝒆𝒕\mathbf{Set}). An operad OO is a sequence of sets {O(n)}n≥0\{O(n)\}_{n \geq 0} together with:

  3. a right action of Σn\Sigma_n on O(n)O(n);

satisfying associativity, unitality, and equivariance.

Type-Theoretic Encoding

We close the categorical setup with the type-theoretic encoding, which is what makes Law I directly executable in a programming language such as Haskell.

Curry–Howard–Lambek

Theorem 46 (Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence ). There is a triangle of equivalences:

  • Cartesian closed categories ↔\;\leftrightarrow\; simply typed λ\lambda-calculi ↔\;\leftrightarrow\; intuitionistic propositional logics.

  • Symmetric monoidal closed categories ↔\;\leftrightarrow\; linear λ\lambda-calculi ↔\;\leftrightarrow\; intuitionistic linear logic.

  • Compact closed categories with dagger ↔\;\leftrightarrow\; quantum λ\lambda-calculi ↔\;\leftrightarrow\; multiplicative linear logic with involutive negation.

For our purposes, the third row is the relevant one: a quantum process is a term of a linear λ\lambda-calculus with involution, equivalently a morphism in a dagger compact closed category.

Linear types and quantum resources

The no-cloning theorem asserts that no quantum channel copy:H→H⊗H\mathrm{copy} : H \to H \otimes H exists for non-trivial HH. Linearly, this is the statement that the type AA has no canonical map A→A⊗AA \to A \otimes A. Linear type systems encode this by forbidding contraction (variable duplication).

Haskell encoding

We encode the categorical hierarchy in Haskell as a sequence of type classes (the full code is supplied in the companion package, 8):

class Category cat where
  cid    :: cat a a
  ccomp  :: cat b c -> cat a b -> cat a c

class Category cat => MonoidalCategory cat where
  type Unit cat
  -- The Haskell pair type (,) realises the monoidal product on
  -- objects. We rely on Haskell's product-type constructor as
  -- a concrete witness of the abstract tensor.
  tensor :: cat a b -> cat c d -> cat (a, c) (b, d)

class Functor1 f where
  fmap1 :: (a -> b) -> f a -> f b

class (Category src, Category tgt) => CFunctor src tgt f where
  -- The dummy method fobj witnesses the type-level object map a -> f a.
  -- See note below regarding phantom encoding.
  fobj :: f a -> ()
  fmor :: src a b -> tgt (f a) (f b)

Concretely realising the monoidal product.

The type signature

tensor :: cat a b -> cat c d -> cat (a,c) (b,d)

uses Haskell’s built-in product type (,) to realise the monoidal product at the type level. This is a deliberate choice: in our concrete instance Fn (Hask + total functions), the categorical product is the Haskell pair, so (a,c) really is the image of a⊗ca \otimes c on objects. For other instances (e.g. a future linearly-typed LinFn) one would replace the pair with a dedicated linear-tensor type constructor, and the corresponding tensor signature would map a pair of morphisms over a,ba, b and c,dc, d to a morphism over the tensors a⊗ca \otimes c and b⊗db \otimes d. The pair-based version above is the cartesian special case.

A note on the encoding of the object component. In Haskell, the object mapping of a functor cannot be made first-class without dependent types: objects of 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} would need to be Haskell types, and a functor’s object map would need to be a type-level function. We follow the standard pragmatic idiom: the type parameter f a carries the object information at the type level (so f a is the image of object a under the functor), and the dummy method fobj :: f a -> () merely witnesses this assignment for the type-class machinery. Readers steeped in pure category theory should read fobj as a phantom artefact of Haskell’s stratification of types and values; the genuine “object component” of the functor is the type-level assignment a↦faa \mapsto f\,a.

Scope and limitations of the Haskell encoding.

The encoding above captures the categorical skeleton of Law I: identity, composition, the monoidal product, and the strong-monoidal functor signature. It does not capture two structural ingredients of 16:

  1. Linearity. Standard Haskell types support unrestricted contraction and weakening (variables can be duplicated and discarded), which is incompatible with the no-cloning and no-deletion theorems of quantum mechanics. Concretely: a Haskell function a -> b can in general duplicate or discard its argument, but if a and b are intended to model quantum states then this would correspond to a copying or deleting channel, which is not a structure-preserving morphism in 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb}. A faithful encoding therefore requires the GHC LinearTypes extension (GHC 9.0+) or a dedicated linear-type framework such as Proto-Quipper-M .

  2. Dagger. The dagger functor †\dagger is involutive and identity-on-objects. Encoding it requires either a wrapper type (Dagger cat a b as a pair of forward and backward morphisms) or a more sophisticated approach using inverse semicategories. Our Dagger-Category type class supplies the signature but does not enforce involutivity at the type level.

Consequently, the executable-encoding hook 𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H8} is realised at the level of the categorical skeleton; full realisation, including linearity and dagger involution, is one of the open problems we list in 9. The property test suite (in-house, QuickCheck-style; see the companion package) verifies the category and monoidal laws on the present skeleton.

The dagger hook 𝖧𝟨\mathsf{H6} is supplied by an additional type class DaggerCategory with method dagger :: cat a b -> cat b a; the Floquet hook 𝖧𝟥\mathsf{H3} is supplied by a one-object category newtype B Z = B Integer together with a functor B Z -> Aut Phys. The matter–information functor is then a Grothendieck construction: given a fibration of categories over 𝒞\mathcal{C}, take fibrewise data.

Example 4: little disks operad on a Hilbert space

Let A∈𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃A \in \mathbf{FHilb} be a vector space carrying an E2E_2-algebra structure (e.g. a vertex operator algebra for Virc\mathrm{Vir}_c). The operadic hook 𝖧𝟧\mathsf{H5} assigns each pair of disjoint disks D1,D2⊂DD_1, D_2 \subset D a multiplication A⊗A→AA \otimes A \to A depending continuously on the configuration. This is the seed of Law IV’s holographic factorisation algebras.

Example 5: Lawvere and linear theories

This example highlights the bits/qubits asymmetry through Lawvere theories and their symmetric monoidal generalisation. A Lawvere theory  is a small category TT with finite products, equipped with a distinguished object XX such that every object is isomorphic to a finite power XnX^n. A model of TT in a category 𝒞\mathcal{C} with finite products is a finite-product-preserving functor T→𝒞T \to \mathcal{C}.

For classical bits, take TBitT_{\mathrm{Bit}} to be the Lawvere theory generated by morphisms copy:X→X×X,xor:X×X→X,0,1:*→X,\mathrm{copy} : X \to X \times X,\qquad \mathrm{xor} : X \times X \to X,\qquad 0, 1 : * \to X, modulo the axioms of Boolean algebra. Models of TBitT_{\mathrm{Bit}} in 𝑺𝒆𝒕\mathbf{Set} are precisely Boolean algebras: a model assigns to XX an underlying set, and to each generator a function compatible with the Boolean axioms.

To extend this picture to quantum bits, one cannot work in a category with finite products, because the diagonal ΔX:X→X×X\Delta_X : X \to X \times X (the universal copying map associated with the categorical product) does not correspond to a quantum channel—its image under any candidate functor TBit→𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃T_{\mathrm{Bit}} \to \mathbf{FHilb} would be a ℂ\mathbb{C}-linear copying map ℂ2→ℂ2⊗ℂ2\mathbb{C}^2 \to \mathbb{C}^2 \otimes \mathbb{C}^2, which is forbidden by no-cloning.

The correct replacement is a symmetric monoidal Lawvere theory, also called a PROP (products and permutations category) in the literature: a small symmetric strict monoidal category whose objects are the natural numbers under addition, generated by a chosen set of morphisms modulo relations. We define:

Definition 49 (Linear Lawvere theory). A linear Lawvere theory, or linear PROP, is a small symmetric strict monoidal category TlinT_\mathrm{lin} whose monoid of objects is (ℕ,+,0)(\mathbb{N}, +, 0), generated by a chosen set of morphisms together with a chosen set of relations among them. A model of TlinT_\mathrm{lin} in a symmetric monoidal category 𝒞\mathcal{C} is a strong symmetric monoidal functor Tlin→𝒞T_\mathrm{lin} \to \mathcal{C}.

Crucially, a linear Lawvere theory has no a priori diagonal: the tensor X⊗XX \otimes X is not a categorical product, and there is no canonical morphism X→X⊗XX \to X \otimes X. To define a copying-style operation one must adjoin a generating morphism, and this adjunction is where the quantum/classical distinction lives: the linear theory TQubitT_{\mathrm{Qubit}} generated only by Hadamard, phase, and CNOT (without a copy generator) embeds faithfully into 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb}, whereas its classical projection (replacing CNOT with copy\mathrm{copy}) does not.

This is the categorical statement of the bits/qubits asymmetry:

  • Classical models live in cartesian categories (𝑺𝒆𝒕\mathbf{Set}, 𝑻𝒐𝒑\mathbf{Top}, etc.) and admit canonical diagonals.

  • Quantum models live in symmetric monoidal (non-cartesian) categories and have no canonical diagonals; the absence of copy\mathrm{copy} is enforced at the categorical level.

The matter–information functor M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info}, when both 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} and 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info} are quantum-style symmetric monoidal categories, automatically respects this absence; when 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info} is a classical environment (e.g. 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐=𝑺𝒆𝒕\mathbf{Info}= \mathbf{Set}), the functor cannot be strong monoidal in the symmetric monoidal sense, and one must pass through a decoherence structure (a commutative dagger Frobenius algebra) to mediate the transition. This example is paradigmatic for how the matter–information correspondence differs from a naive set-valued semantics.

Open Problems

We list four open problems that the foundational layer (Law I) leaves to future work, in roughly increasing order of ambition:

  1. Renormalisation as a categorical structure. Atiyah’s TQFT axioms work for finite-dimensional models. A categorical axiomatisation of QFT that handles infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and renormalisation, perhaps via Costello–Gwilliam factorisation algebras, remains an open problem of foundational interest .

  2. Linear dependent type theory and measurement. A type theory with linear dependent types that is computationally tractable and sound for categorical quantum mechanics with measurements would close the type-theoretic hook 𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H8} at the level needed for Laws III–IV .

  3. Cohesive HoTT vs. factorisation algebras. The relationship between Schreiber–Shulman’s cohesive HoTT  and the factorisation-algebra approach to QFT  for flat spacetimes is conjectured but not proven equivalent.

  4. Born rule beyond Gleason. A purely categorical characterisation of the Born rule, beyond the existing assumption of compact closure with dagger and beyond Gleason’s theorem on lattices of projections, remains a foundational target .

Discussion

What Law I does and does not claim

Law I claims a structural fact: if matter and information are organised as objects of suitably structured symmetric monoidal categories, then their relation is forced to be a strong monoidal dagger functor satisfying the conditions of 16. The hooks 𝖧𝟣\mathsf{H1}–𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H8} are slots; they are not filled by Law I.

Law I does not claim:

  • that the categorical formulation is unique. There are alternative formulations (e.g. algebraic QFT in the Haag–Kastler sense, or operator algebraic settings).

  • that all of physics is captured. We address only the matter–information correspondence layer; dynamics, gravity, and renormalisation are addressed in the subsequent laws.

  • that the framework is complete. Many of the open problems in 9 are precisely about completion.

Comparison with related programmes

The framework here is closest in spirit to the categorical quantum mechanics programme of Abramsky–Coecke  and the higher-categorical TQFT programme of Lurie . The novelty is the explicit declaration of composition hooks: rather than presenting a self-contained mathematical edifice, we present a structured interface designed to be filled by downstream laws. This methodological choice is what makes the programme modular rather than unified.

Categorical entropy and the bridge to information

A natural objection to the present formulation is that it does not visibly contain entropy or any quantitative measure of information. We respond by recalling the categorical characterisation of Shannon entropy due to Baez–Fritz–Leinster: Shannon entropy is the unique (up to scale) functor H:𝑭𝒊𝒏𝑷𝒓𝒐𝒃→[0,∞)H : \mathbf{FinProb} \to [0,\infty) satisfying functoriality, the chain rule, and continuity, where 𝑭𝒊𝒏𝑷𝒓𝒐𝒃\mathbf{FinProb} is the category of finite probability spaces and measure-preserving maps. This characterisation lifts to 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info} provided 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info} contains a sub-monoidal category equivalent to 𝑭𝒊𝒏𝑷𝒓𝒐𝒃\mathbf{FinProb}.

Remark 50 (Information measures as functors). The slogan “information is functorial” is realised concretely: every reasonable information-theoretic quantity (Shannon, Rényi-α\alpha, von Neumann, conditional, mutual, relative) admits a categorical characterisation. Law IV uses these characterisations to reduce holographic entanglement-entropy formulas to functorial identities.

Connection to the cobordism hypothesis as “representability”

12 (Cobordism Hypothesis) plays a methodologically distinctive role in our framework: it is a representability theorem. It says that every symmetric monoidal (∞,n)(\infty,n)-functor out of 𝑩𝒐𝒓𝒅nfr\mathbf{Bord}_n^{\mathrm{fr}} is represented by its value at the point. We invoke this representability to argue that the matter–information functor MM is determined by very little data—in the cobordism case, by the single object M(pt)M(\mathrm{pt}), which must be fully dualisable.

This has an immediate methodological corollary: when working with a fixed category 𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔\mathbf{Phys} that admits a presentation as a free symmetric monoidal (∞,n)(\infty,n)-category with duals, the matter–information functor is fixed by the specification of a single fully-dualisable object in 𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐\mathbf{Info}. Downstream laws use this principle to constrain their constructions: Law II’s phase classifier, Law III’s Floquet operator, and Law IV’s holographic code each amount to a choice of fully-dualisable object satisfying additional constraints.

Modular composition, not unification

Throughout, we have insisted on modular language. The four-law programme is not a unified theory; it is a stack of layers, each layer self-standing, with composition controlled by functors and natural transformations. The reader who finds, say, Law III’s Floquet category problematic can in principle replace it with another category satisfying the hook 𝖧𝟥\mathsf{H3}, and the rest of the programme continues to apply. This is the engineering virtue of the modular approach.

Conclusion

We have introduced Law I of a four-paper modular programme: the categorical foundations for matter–information correspondence. The contributions are:

  1. A precise definition of the matter–information functor M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info} as a strong monoidal dagger functor.

  2. Three structural results: generation of MM from a presentation (18), a Born-rule type formula from compact closure (20), and sheaf lifting through MM (39).

  3. Eight composition hooks 𝖧𝟣\mathsf{H1}–𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H8} that downstream laws (II–IV) plug into, with explicit signature and intended semantics.

  4. A Haskell encoding of the categorical skeleton with QuickCheck property tests for the category and monoidal laws.

  5. Worked examples drawn from 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb}, the cobordism category, presheaves on a poset, and the little-disks operad.

The next paper in the series, Law II (Phase-bound Matter), uses these hooks (particularly 𝖧𝟣,𝖧𝟤,𝖧𝟦,𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H1}, \mathsf{H2}, \mathsf{H4}, \mathsf{H8}) to classify thermodynamic and topological phases as functors over symmetry-group categories.

Compositional roadmap

The four-paper modular series composes its laws in the following order:

  1. Law I (this paper): Categorical primitives. Output: hooks 𝖧𝟣\mathsf{H1}–𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H8} and a matter–information functor M:𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔→𝑰𝒏𝒇𝒐M : \mathbf{Phys}\to \mathbf{Info}.

  2. Law II (Phase-bound Matter): Plug 𝖧𝟣,𝖧𝟤,𝖧𝟦,𝖧𝟪\mathsf{H1}, \mathsf{H2}, \mathsf{H4}, \mathsf{H8}. Output: a functor Phase:BG→𝑯𝒂𝒎/∼adiabatic\mathrm{Phase} : B G \to \mathbf{Ham} / \!\sim_{\mathrm{adiabatic}} classifying gapped phases.

  3. Law III (Frequency-modulated Processes): Plug 𝖧𝟥,𝖧𝟨\mathsf{H3}, \mathsf{H6}. Output: a Floquet functor F:Bℤ→𝑷𝒉𝒚𝒔F : B \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbf{Phys} and its natural-transformation classifications. Composes with Law II to produce non-equilibrium phase classifiers.

  4. Law IV (Information-bearing Structures): Plug 𝖧𝟧,𝖧𝟩\mathsf{H5}, \mathsf{H7}. Output: a holographic code (HaPPY-type) realised as a fully-dualisable object whose tensor-network geometry computes Ryu–Takayanagi-style entanglement entropies.

The synthesis paper then exhibits the composite of all four laws, in which each layer’s hook is realised by the layer above. The composition is not unique—different physical systems give different fillings of the same hooks—but the type of the composite is fixed by Law I.

What a reader of Law I should take away

Three concrete takeaways are intended:

  • The matter–information correspondence is the type of a structure-preserving functor between two symmetric monoidal dagger categories. Anything compatible with this type is a candidate matter–information correspondence.

  • Eight composition hooks give precise interfaces for downstream layers. A reader implementing a new layer should ask: which hooks does it consume, and which does it leave untouched?

  • The framework is modular, not unified. Replacing any single layer with a different filling of its hook leaves the rest of the framework intact, because the only contract between layers is the typed hook signature.
