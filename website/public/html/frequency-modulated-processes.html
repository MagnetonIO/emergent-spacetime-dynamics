Introduction

Position in the modular series

This is Paper 3 of the four-paper modular research programme Emergent Spacetime Dynamics. The series formalises a hierarchy of emergent phenomena in quantum many-body systems through category theory, organised as a sequence of compositional liftings: 𝖢𝖺𝗍mon→LiftI→II𝖯𝗁𝖺𝗌𝖾eq→LiftII→III𝖯𝗁𝖺𝗌𝖾Floq→LiftIII→IV𝖨𝗇𝖿𝗈𝖦𝖾𝗈𝗆.\begin{equation} \label{eq:lifting-tower} \mathsf{Cat}_{\mathrm{mon}} \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{Lift}_{\mathrm{I}\to\mathrm{II}}\;} \mathsf{Phase}_{\mathrm{eq}} \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{Lift}_{\mathrm{II}\to\mathrm{III}}\;} \mathsf{Phase}_{\mathrm{Floq}} \xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{Lift}_{\mathrm{III}\to\mathrm{IV}}\;} \mathsf{InfoGeom}. \end{equation} We stress at the outset that this is a modular framework, not a unified one: each lift is a distinct functor, and the emergent properties at each stage are produced by the composition itself rather than reducible to any single prior layer. The third paper, presented here, covers the lift from equilibrium-phase functors to Floquet-phase functors; that is, it extends the categorical phase-classification machinery of Law II by one monoidal dimension — time — producing as emergent phenomena: (a) discrete time-crystalline order, (b) anomalous Floquet topological insulators with no equilibrium analog, and (c) prethermal Floquet engineering of effective Hamiltonians.

Recap of Laws I and II

Law I: Categorical Primitives.

Law I established the shared categorical grammar: symmetric monoidal categories (SMCs) , dagger-compact categories , sheaves, topoi , operads, and their type-theoretic encodings. The central object is the SMC (𝒞,⊗,I)(\mathcal{C},\otimes,I) together with structural natural isomorphisms (associator, unitors, braiding) satisfying Mac Lane coherence . Finite-dimensional quantum mechanics (which suffices for our lattice-model setting) is the dagger-compact category 𝖥𝖧𝗂𝗅𝖻\mathsf{FHilb} of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces ; processes are morphisms; tensor product is system composition; and string diagrams are sound and complete by Joyal–Street. Law I distilled the slogan: a physical theory is a symmetric monoidal functor Z:𝒞geo→𝖥𝖧𝗂𝗅𝖻Z\colon \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}} \to \mathsf{FHilb}, with 𝒞geo\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{geo}} a category of geometric or process data; the prototypical example is Atiyah’s TQFT  and its extended cousins .

Law II: Phase-bound Matter.

Law II used the Law I grammar to classify equilibrium phases of matter as functors from a symmetry-group category to a category of gapped Hamiltonians, equipped with morphisms given by adiabatic deformations. Concretely, a phase with on-site symmetry group GG is a homotopy class of functors F:𝑩G→𝖧𝖺𝗆gap,\begin{equation} F\colon \mathbf{B}G \longrightarrow \mathsf{Ham}_{\mathrm{gap}}, \end{equation} where 𝑩G\mathbf{B}G is the one-object delooping. Symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases are classified by the (cobordism-) cohomology of 𝑩G\mathbf{B}G ; topologically ordered phases are classified by modular tensor categories (MTCs) ; a phase transition is a natural transformation that fails to be invertible at the Hamiltonian level (the gap closes).

What Law III adds

Law III adjoins to Law II a single new categorical dimension: a ℤT\mathbb{Z}_T-grading representing periodic time evolution. The Schrödinger equation with a TT-periodic Hamiltonian H(t+T)=H(t)H(t+T)=H(t) produces, by the Floquet theorem , a one-cycle unitary U(T)=𝒯exp⁡(−i∫0TH(t)dt)U(T)= \mathcal{T}\exp\bigl(-i\!\int_0^T H(t)\,dt\bigr) whose spectrum (the quasi-energy spectrum) lives on a torus S1=(−π/T,π/T]S^1=(-\pi/T,\pi/T] rather than on the real line. Functorially, this means Floquet evolution is best understood as a monoidal functor from the discrete circle category to the category of quantum channels.

The framework yields three emergent phenomena that are absent from Law II:

  1. Discrete time crystals (DTCs) : spontaneous breaking of the discrete time-translation symmetry of the drive, manifested as a local order parameter oscillating at nTnT for some integer n>1n>1.

  2. Anomalous Floquet topological insulators : phases with chiral edge modes at quasi-energy π\pi despite vanishing Chern numbers in every band, classified by a ℤ\mathbb{Z}-valued Floquet winding number on BZ×S1\mathrm{BZ}\times S^1.

  3. Prethermal Floquet engineering : an exponentially long time window during which the dynamics is governed by an effective Hamiltonian HeffH_{\mathrm{eff}} obtained by truncating the Magnus expansion, allowing engineered phases not present in the parent static system.

The classification of Floquet topological phases by KK-theory  is the temporal analog of Kitaev’s 1010-fold way; we recover it categorically in 6.

Mathematical Framework: Floquet Theory in Categorical Form

The discrete circle category

Definition 1 (Discrete circle category). Fix a period T>0T>0. The discrete circle category at scale TT, denoted 𝑩ℤT\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_T, is the delooping of the integers ℤ\mathbb{Z} decorated by the parameter TT: it has a single object ** and morphism set Hom(*,*)=ℤ\mathrm{Hom}(*,*) = \mathbb{Z}, with composition being addition. The subscript TT is not a modulus and does not impose modular arithmetic on ℤ\mathbb{Z}; it is a metadatum recording the period that the generating morphism 1∈ℤ1\in\mathbb{Z} is meant to represent (i.e. “one period of duration TT”). Equivalently, 𝑩ℤT\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_T is the delooping of π1(S1)≅ℤ\pi_1(S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z} viewed as the discrete-time-translation group of a continuous circle of circumference TT. We write 𝑩ℤT+\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_T^+ for the wide subcategory generated by the positive integers (forward time only).

Remark 2 (On the notation ℤT\mathbb{Z}_T). The subscript TT in ℤT\mathbb{Z}_T is a notational decoration recording the period TT as a parameter, not a quotient. We use this slightly non-standard convention because the period TT enters the categorical Floquet picture only through its action on the morphisms (each morphism n∈ℤn\in\mathbb{Z} stands for “nn stroboscopic periods of duration TT”), not through any modular reduction. Where modular arithmetic is genuinely intended in the paper (e.g. in ℝ/Tℤ\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z} for the continuum circle), we write the quotient explicitly.

Definition 3 (Continuous-time circle category). The continuous-time circle category 𝑩S1\mathbf{B}S^1 has one object and morphism space S1=ℝ/TℤS^1=\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z} with composition given by addition modulo TT. As a topological category, 𝑩S1\mathbf{B}S^1 has trivial π0\pi_0 and π1(𝑩S1)≅ℤ\pi_1(\mathbf{B}S^1)\cong\mathbb{Z} encoding winding number.

The continuum-time analog above uses the topological group S1=ℝ/TℤS^1=\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z} delooped to the topological category 𝑩S1\mathbf{B}S^1.

The category of quantum channels

We work in the dagger-symmetric monoidal category 𝖰𝖢𝗁𝖺𝗇\mathsf{QChan} of finite-dimensional quantum channels. Objects are finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces ℋ\mathcal{H}; morphisms ℋ→ℋ′\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}' are completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) maps. Tensor product is the standard tensor of Hilbert spaces and Stinespring dilation of channels; the dagger is given by the adjoint channel under the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product.

We will at times restrict to the wide subcategory 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍⊂𝖰𝖢𝗁𝖺𝗇\mathsf{Unit}\subset\mathsf{QChan} of unitary channels, which is itself a dagger-monoidal subcategory. A unitary channel is the conjugation ρ↦UρU†\rho\mapsto U\rho U^\dagger for some unitary UU.

The Floquet functor

Definition 4 (Floquet functor). A Floquet system on a Hilbert space ℋ\mathcal{H} with period TT is a strong monoidal functor 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊:𝑩ℤT→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍⊂𝖰𝖢𝗁𝖺𝗇\begin{equation} \mathsf{Floq}\colon \mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_T \longrightarrow \mathsf{Unit}\subset\mathsf{QChan} \end{equation} sending the generating morphism 1∈ℤ=Hom(*,*)1\in\mathbb{Z}=\mathrm{Hom}(*,*) to a unitary channel 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊(1)=𝒰T:ℋ→ℋ\mathsf{Floq}(1)=\mathcal{U}_T\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}, where 𝒰T(ρ)=U(T)ρU(T)†\mathcal{U}_T(\rho)=U(T)\,\rho\,U(T)^\dagger for the one-period evolution operator U(T)U(T).

Remark 5. 4 captures only the stroboscopic content of a Floquet system; the micromotion is recovered by lifting to the continuous-time category. The full Floquet system is a continuous functor 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊̃:𝑩S1→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\widetilde{\mathsf{Floq}}\colon\mathbf{B}S^1\to\mathsf{Unit}, whose restriction along the discretisation 𝑩ℤT↪𝑩S1\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_T\hookrightarrow\mathbf{B}S^1 recovers 4.

Theorem 6 (Floquet decomposition, categorical form). Let 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊̃:𝑩S1→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\widetilde{\mathsf{Floq}}\colon\mathbf{B}S^1\to\mathsf{Unit} be a continuous Floquet functor. Then there exist:

  1. a unitary endomorphism UF=e−iHFTU_F=e^{-iH_{F}T} (the stroboscopic Floquet operator), uniquely determined by 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊̃\widetilde{\mathsf{Floq}} up to multiplication of HFH_{F} by integer multiples of ω=2π/T\omega = 2\pi/T,

  2. a continuous family P:S1→End(ℋ)P\colon S^1\to\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H}) of unitary operators with P(0)=𝟏P(0)=\mathbf{1} (the micromotion), such that

𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊̃(t)(ρ)=P(t)e−iHFtρeiHFtP(t)†∀t∈ℝ/Tℤ.\begin{equation} \widetilde{\mathsf{Floq}}(t)(\rho) = P(t)\,e^{-iH_{F}t}\,\rho\,e^{iH_{F}t}\,P(t)^\dagger \qquad \forall t\in\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}. \end{equation}

Proof sketch. This is the operator-level statement of the classical Floquet theorem . Continuity of 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊̃\widetilde{\mathsf{Floq}} furnishes a continuous evolution U(t,0)U(t,0) with U(T,0)U(T,0) unitary. The functorial wrapping in 𝖰𝖢𝗁𝖺𝗇\mathsf{QChan} is just the conjugation action. ◻

Sambe space and the spectral side

The quasi-energy spectrum lives on the Pontryagin dual ℤ̂=S1\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}=S^1. To work with quasi-energies as a self-adjoint operator spectrum, we introduce the Sambe space .

Definition 7 (Sambe space). Let ℋ\mathcal{H} be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and T>0T>0. The Sambe space associated to the period TT is the (separable) Hilbert space 𝒮T(ℋ):=L2(ST1)⊗ℋ,\begin{equation} \mathcal{S}_T(\mathcal{H}) \;:=\; L^2(S^1_T)\otimes\mathcal{H}, \end{equation} where ST1=ℝ/TℤS^1_T = \mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z} with normalised Haar measure dt/Tdt/T. Its Fourier basis {en=einωt}n∈ℤ\{e_n=e^{i n\omega t}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}} decomposes 𝒮T(ℋ)=⨁n∈ℤℋn\mathcal{S}_T(\mathcal{H})=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{H}_n with each ℋn≅ℋ\mathcal{H}_n\cong\mathcal{H}.

Definition 8 (Quasi-energy operator). For a smooth TT-periodic curve H:ST1→Herm(ℋ)H\colon S^1_T\to\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}), the quasi-energy operator on 𝒮T(ℋ)\mathcal{S}_T(\mathcal{H}) is K:=H(t)−i∂t.\begin{equation} K \;:=\; H(t)\;-\;i\partial_t. \end{equation} Its eigenvalues {εα+nω:α,n}\{\varepsilon_\alpha+n\omega : \alpha,n\} are organised in ℤ\mathbb{Z}-shifted ladders, and its eigenvectors ψα=e−iεαtuα(t)\psi_\alpha=e^{-i\varepsilon_\alpha t}u_\alpha(t) are the Floquet modes.

Proposition 9 (Sambe-space functor). The assignment ℋ↦𝒮T(ℋ)\mathcal{H}\mapsto\mathcal{S}_T(\mathcal{H}), f↦𝟏⊗ff\mapsto\mathbf{1}\otimes f extends to a strong monoidal functor 𝒮T:𝖰𝖢𝗁𝖺𝗇fin→𝖧𝗂𝗅𝖻sep,\begin{equation} \mathcal{S}_T\colon \mathsf{QChan}_{\mathrm{fin}} \longrightarrow \mathsf{Hilb}_{\mathrm{sep}}, \end{equation} that intertwines the Floquet functor 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊\mathsf{Floq} with the diagonal action of KK on 𝒮T(ℋ)\mathcal{S}_T(\mathcal{H}).

Proof. Strong monoidality. We work with the natural lax-monoidal structure for which the structural map μℋ,ℋ′:𝒮T(ℋ)⊗L2(ST1)𝒮T(ℋ′)→≅𝒮T(ℋ⊗ℋ′)\begin{equation*} \mu_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}'}\colon \mathcal{S}_T(\mathcal{H})\otimes_{L^2(S^1_T)}\mathcal{S}_T(\mathcal{H}')\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;\mathcal{S}_T(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}') \end{equation*} sends a simple tensor (f⊗ψ)⊗L2(ST1)(g⊗ψ′)(f\otimes\psi)\otimes_{L^2(S^1_T)}(g\otimes\psi') to (fg)⊗(ψ⊗ψ′)(fg)\otimes(\psi\otimes\psi'), where fgfg denotes pointwise multiplication of f,g∈L2(ST1)∩C∞(ST1)f,g\in L^2(S^1_T)\cap C^\infty(S^1_T) (the smooth subspace, which is dense). Here we view 𝖧𝗂𝗅𝖻sep\mathsf{Hilb}_{\mathrm{sep}} as enriched-monoidal over the commutative C*C^*-algebra L2(ST1)∩L∞(ST1)L^2(S^1_T)\cap L^\infty(S^1_T), and the relative tensor product ⊗L2(ST1)\otimes_{L^2(S^1_T)} is the standard module tensor over this algebra. With this convention, μℋ,ℋ′\mu_{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}'} is well-defined on the dense subspace of simple tensors of smooth functions and finite-rank Hilbert-space vectors, and extends by continuity (in the natural Hilbert-space norm on the relative tensor product) to a unitary isomorphism on the full tensor product space. The pentagon axiom for μ\mu reduces to associativity of pointwise multiplication of smooth functions tensored with associativity of the standard ⊗\otimes on Hilbert spaces; the triangle axiom is similarly straightforward. (If the relative tensor product is replaced by the bare tensor ⊗\otimes, then μ\mu becomes lax-monoidal rather than strong-monoidal, with the failure measured by an L2(ST1)L^2(S^1_T)-valued density; the proposition holds in either form, but only the relative-tensor version is strongly monoidal.).

Intertwining of 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊̃\widetilde{\mathsf{Floq}} with KK. By 6, the continuous Floquet functor 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊̃\widetilde{\mathsf{Floq}} acts on the standard Hilbert space ℋ\mathcal{H} by conjugation with U(t,0)=P(t)e−iHFtU(t,0)=P(t)e^{-iH_{F}t}. Under this identification:

  1. The quasi-energy operator K=H(t)−i∂tK = H(t) - i\partial_t is diagonal on each en⊗uαe_n\otimes u_\alpha, with eigenvalue εα+nω\varepsilon_\alpha + n\omega. This is the standard ladder structure of Sambe space .

Periodic Hamiltonians as Endomorphisms

The category of TT-periodic Hamiltonians

Definition 10 (Periodic-Hamiltonian category). Fix a period T>0T>0. The category 𝖧𝖺𝗆T\mathsf{Ham}_T has:

  • objects: pairs (ℋ,H)(\mathcal{H},H) where ℋ\mathcal{H} is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space and H:ST1→Herm(ℋ)H\colon S^1_T\to\mathrm{Herm}(\mathcal{H}) is a smooth TT-periodic curve of self-adjoint operators;

  • morphisms (ℋ,H)→(ℋ′,H′)(\mathcal{H},H)\to(\mathcal{H}',H'): linear isometries V:ℋ→ℋ′V\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}' such that VH(t)=H′(t)VVH(t)=H'(t)V for all tt.

The category 𝖧𝖺𝗆T\mathsf{Ham}_T is symmetric monoidal: the tensor product of objects (ℋ1,H1)(\mathcal{H}_1,H_1) and (ℋ2,H2)(\mathcal{H}_2,H_2) is the pair (ℋ1⊗ℋ2,H1(t)⊗𝟏+𝟏⊗H2(t))(\mathcal{H}_1\otimes\mathcal{H}_2,\,H_1(t)\otimes\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}\otimes H_2(t)), with structural isomorphisms inherited from 𝖧𝗂𝗅𝖻\mathsf{Hilb}.

Floquet evolution as a strong monoidal functor

Definition 11 (The graded unitary category 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}}). The ℤ\mathbb{Z}-graded unitary category 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}} has

  • objects: finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces ℋ\mathcal{H};

  • morphisms ℋ→ℋ′\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}': pairs (n,W)(n,W) with n∈ℤn\in\mathbb{Z} and W:ℋ→ℋ′W\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}' a unitary linear map (in particular, ℋ\mathcal{H} and ℋ′\mathcal{H}' have the same dimension whenever a morphism exists between them);

  • identity: idℋ=(0,𝟏ℋ)\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{H}}=(0,\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}});

  • composition of (m,W′):ℋ′→ℋ″(m,W')\colon\mathcal{H}'\to\mathcal{H}'' with (n,W):ℋ→ℋ′(n,W)\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}' is (m,W′)∘(n,W):=(n+m,W′W)(m,W')\circ(n,W) := (n+m,\,W'\,W).

The integer label nn records the number of stroboscopic periods that the morphism represents; it is additive under composition and trivial on identities. The category 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}} inherits a symmetric monoidal structure from the standard tensor product of Hilbert spaces: on objects, ℋ⊗ℋ′\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}' is the usual tensor; on morphisms, (n,W)⊗(n′,W′):=(n+n′,W⊗W′)\begin{equation*} (n,W)\otimes(n',W') := (n+n',\,W\otimes W') \end{equation*} i.e. the ℤ\mathbb{Z}-grading on a tensor product of morphisms is the sum of the gradings (consistent with the additive structure of ℤ\mathbb{Z} under composition), and the underlying unitaries tensor in the usual way. Equivalently, 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}} is 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\mathsf{Unit} enriched over the discrete strict monoidal category associated with (ℤ,+,0)(\mathbb{Z},+,0).

To make the period-counting structure of Floquet evolution functorial, we should not interpret intertwining isometries between distinct Floquet systems as carrying an a-priori period count. Instead, an intertwiner is a static identification of two systems and naturally has period count 00; the period count is a separate piece of data, generated by the cyclic action of the Floquet operator on each fixed system. We make this precise as follows.

Theorem 12 (Floquet evolution as a functor to graded unitaries). The Floquet evolution data assemble into a strong monoidal functor EvT:𝖧𝖺𝗆T→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\begin{equation} \mathrm{Ev}_T\colon \mathsf{Ham}_T \longrightarrow \mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}} \end{equation} defined on objects by EvT(ℋ,H)=ℋ\mathrm{Ev}_T(\mathcal{H},H)=\mathcal{H} and on morphisms by EvT(V)=(0,V)\mathrm{Ev}_T(V)=(0,V) for every intertwining isometry V:(ℋ,H)→(ℋ′,H′)V\colon(\mathcal{H},H)\to(\mathcal{H}',H') (including V=𝟏V=\mathbf{1}, which is sent to (0,𝟏)(0,\mathbf{1}) as the identity of ℋ\mathcal{H}).

The Floquet operator data are encoded as an additional natural transformation Φ:EvT⇒EvT,\begin{equation} \Phi\colon \mathrm{Ev}_T \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathrm{Ev}_T, \end{equation} whose component at (ℋ,H)(\mathcal{H},H) is the morphism (1,UF(H))∈Hom𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ(ℋ,ℋ)(1,U_F^{(H)})\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}), where UF(H)=UH(T,0)U_F^{(H)}=U_H(T,0) is the one-period stroboscopic operator of HH. Naturality of Φ\Phi at an intertwiner VV is the relation (0,V)∘(1,UF(H))=(1,UF(H′))∘(0,V),i.e.VUF(H)=UF(H′)V,\begin{equation} (0,V)\circ(1,U_F^{(H)}) \;=\; (1,U_F^{(H')})\circ(0,V),\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad VU_F^{(H)}=U_F^{(H')}V, \end{equation} which holds because VV intertwines H(t)H(t) and H′(t)H'(t) pointwise in tt, hence intertwines their time-ordered exponentials.

Remark 13 (Why intertwiners carry grading 00). A morphism in 𝖧𝖺𝗆T\mathsf{Ham}_T is a static identification of two periodic Hamiltonian systems, not a time evolution. Its image under EvT\mathrm{Ev}_T accordingly sits in the grading-00 part of 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}}, where “no period elapses”. The temporal cyclic generator (the Floquet operator) is encoded by the natural transformation Φ\Phi, which produces grading-11 endomorphisms of each individual object. Iterations Φ∘k\Phi^{\circ k} have grading kk, so the period-counting structure lives entirely in the natural-transformation slot, separate from the static categorical structure of 𝖧𝖺𝗆T\mathsf{Ham}_T. This separation is exactly the categorical analog of the operator-vs.-state distinction in physics: the Floquet operator UFU_F is a dynamical generator, distinct from a structural identification of two systems.

Proof of 12. Functoriality of EvT\mathrm{Ev}_T. On objects EvT\mathrm{Ev}_T is well-defined. On morphisms, EvT(id)=(0,𝟏)\mathrm{Ev}_T(\mathrm{id})=(0,\mathbf{1}) in 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}}, which matches the identity. For composition, EvT(V′∘V)=(0,V′V)=(0,V′)∘(0,V)=EvT(V′)∘EvT(V),\begin{equation*} \mathrm{Ev}_T(V'\circ V)=(0,V'V)=(0,V')\circ(0,V)=\mathrm{Ev}_T(V')\circ\mathrm{Ev}_T(V), \end{equation*} where the middle equality uses the composition rule of 11. Thus EvT\mathrm{Ev}_T is a functor.

Naturality of Φ\Phi. Endomorphisms and stroboscopic dynamics

Restricting to a single Hilbert space, 𝖧𝖺𝗆T\mathsf{Ham}_T becomes a one-object category whose endomorphisms are TT-periodic Hamiltonians and whose composition is conjugation by the evolution. The image under EvT\mathrm{Ev}_T is then a one-object subcategory of 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}}, that is, a ℤ\mathbb{Z}-graded group of unitaries: stroboscopic dynamics is generated by U(T)U(T), and the integer label tracks the number of periods. This is the precise sense in which a Floquet system is an endomorphism in the categorical framework.

Remark 14. The ℤ\mathbb{Z}-grading on 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤ\mathsf{Unit}_{\mathbb{Z}} is essential: it is what distinguishes Floquet evolution from a static one-shot evolution. Forgetting the grading produces the underlying static functor; recalling it brings back the temporal structure that supports Floquet phases.

Compositional lifting from Law II

The crucial structural fact is that the prethermal regime allows us to view a Floquet phase as a Law II equilibrium phase of HeffH_{\mathrm{eff}}.

In what follows we use the symbol 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊(−)\mathsf{Floq}_{(-)} to denote the Floquet phase-classification functor with source 𝖧𝖺𝗆T\mathsf{Ham}_T (rather than the bare Floquet system 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊\mathsf{Floq} of 4, whose source is 𝑩ℤT\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_T). Concretely, 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊(−)\mathsf{Floq}_{(-)} takes a TT-periodic Hamiltonian (ℋ,H)(\mathcal{H},H) and returns the connected component of its stroboscopic operator UF(H)U_F^{(H)} within the moduli space of gapped Floquet operators, i.e. its Floquet phase class. Similarly, 𝖯𝗁𝖺𝗌𝖾𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊\mathsf{Phase}_{\mathsf{Floq}} denotes the discrete category of Floquet phase classes (objects: connected components of gapped Floquet operators; morphisms: identity components and inclusions of stratified strata).

Theorem 21 (Prethermal compositional lifting). Proof sketch. For times t≤τ*=ecω/Jt\leq\tau^*=e^{c\omega/J}, the Floquet evolution is approximated by e−iHeff(≤N)te^{-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(\leq N)} t} to within an error of order (J/ω)N(J/\omega)^N. 21 is the precise sense in which Law III lifts Law II: prethermal Floquet phases are equilibrium phases of an effective Hamiltonian, while anomalous Floquet phases are obstructions to the lift.

Discrete Time-Crystal Phases

Definition and physical setup

Definition 22 (Discrete time crystal). A discrete time crystal (DTC) of order n≥2n\geq 2 is a Floquet system 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊:𝑩ℤT→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\mathsf{Floq}\colon\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_T\to\mathsf{Unit} together with a local order parameter 𝒪x\mathcal{O}_x (Hermitian, supported in a region around site xx) and a state |ψ0⟩|\psi_0\rangle such that:

  1. Spontaneous time-translation symmetry breaking: ⟨ψ0|𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊(k)𝒪x𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊(k)†|ψ0⟩\langle\psi_0|\mathsf{Floq}(k)\mathcal{O}_x\mathsf{Floq}(k)^\dagger|\psi_0\rangle has period exactly nTnT in the stroboscopic step kk, even though 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊\mathsf{Floq} has period TT.

  2. Long-range order: the value of the order parameter does not decay in the thermodynamic limit, lim⁡L→∞|⟨𝒪x⟩|>0\lim_{L\to\infty}|\langle\mathcal{O}_x\rangle|>0.

  3. Robustness: the period-nTnT oscillation persists under generic small perturbations of the drive that respect TT-periodicity.

The seminal construction  uses an MBL-stabilised Ising chain (8). Experimental realisations are reported in .

DTC as an obstruction 22-cell

We now state our central categorical result for DTCs. Throughout this subsection, we work in the setting where the system has a global discrete symmetry ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n acting unitarily by some operator SS commuting with the drive. The drive symmetry partitions the Hilbert space into a direct sum of symmetry sectors indexed by ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n-characters, and the drive can spontaneously break ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n to a smaller subgroup.

Definition 23 (Iterated and restricted Floquet functors). Let 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊:𝑩ℤT→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\mathsf{Floq}\colon\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_T\to\mathsf{Unit} be a Floquet system with stroboscopic operator UFU_F. We define two associated Floquet functors:

  1. the iterated Floquet functor 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊[n]:𝑩ℤnT→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\mathsf{Floq}^{[n]}\colon\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_{nT}\to\mathsf{Unit} that sends the generator of ℤ=Hom𝑩ℤnT(*,*)\mathbb{Z}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_{nT}}(*,*) to the unitary channel ρ↦UFnρ(UFn)†\rho\mapsto U_F^n\,\rho\,(U_F^n)^\dagger;

  2. the restricted Floquet functor ResnT𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊:𝑩ℤnT→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\mathrm{Res}_{nT}\mathsf{Floq}\colon\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_{nT}\to\mathsf{Unit} obtained by restriction along the inclusion 𝑩(nℤ)↪𝑩ℤ\mathbf{B}(n\mathbb{Z})\hookrightarrow\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z} and the canonical re-labelling that views an nn-fold iterated TT-periodic dynamics as a single morphism in 𝑩ℤnT\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_{nT}.

On objects (Hilbert spaces) both functors agree; on morphisms they both send the generator of ℤ\mathbb{Z} to a unitary channel implemented by UFnU_F^n. The two functors are therefore literally equal as functors of plain (forgetful) data.

The point is now that to detect a DTC one must enrich both functors with the data of the global symmetry SS and ask whether they agree as functors landing in the symmetry-equivariant subcategory 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤn⊂𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\mathsf{Unit}^{\mathbb{Z}_n}\subset\mathsf{Unit}.

Definition 24 (Symmetry-equivariant Floquet functors). Let S∈U(ℋ)S\in U(\mathcal{H}) implement a ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n symmetry of the drive (SH(t)=H(t)SSH(t)=H(t)S for all tt). Define the symmetry-equivariant lifts 𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊S[n],ResnT𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊S:𝑩ℤnT→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤn,\begin{align} \mathsf{Floq}^{[n]}_S,\;\mathrm{Res}_{nT}\mathsf{Floq}^S\colon\mathbf{B}\mathbb{Z}_{nT}\;&\longrightarrow\;\mathsf{Unit}^{\mathbb{Z}_n}, \end{align} where 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤn\mathsf{Unit}^{\mathbb{Z}_n} is the category whose objects are pairs (ℋ,S)(\mathcal{H}, S) of a Hilbert space and a ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n-symmetry, and whose morphisms are unitaries that commute with SS. The first sends the generator of ℤ\mathbb{Z} to UFnU_F^n together with SS; the second sends it to UFnU_F^n together with the stroboscopically-induced symmetry S′=UFSUF−1S' = U_F S U_F^{-1} (which need not equal SS when the drive permutes symmetry sectors).

Theorem 25 (DTC obstruction). Let (𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊,S)(\mathsf{Floq}, S) be a Floquet system with ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n-symmetry SS. The system realises a discrete time crystal of order nn if and only if the canonical natural transformation η:ResnT𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊S⇒𝖥𝗅𝗈𝗊S[n]\begin{equation} \eta\colon \mathrm{Res}_{nT}\mathsf{Floq}^S \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathsf{Floq}^{[n]}_S \end{equation} (whose component at the unique object is the unitary UFnU_F^n, intended to identify the two pairs (ℋ,UFSUF−1)(\mathcal{H},U_F SU_F^{-1}) and (ℋ,S)(\mathcal{H},S)) fails to be an isomorphism in 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤn\mathsf{Unit}^{\mathbb{Z}_n}. The failure occurs precisely because the two source/target pairs in the enriched category 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤn\mathsf{Unit}^{\mathbb{Z}_n} have different symmetry-generator data (UFSUF−1U_F SU_F^{-1} versus SS), so any prospective component UFnU_F^n does not commute with the relevant symmetry data, breaking the equivariance condition required to be an isomorphism in 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤn\mathsf{Unit}^{\mathbb{Z}_n}. Equivalently, the relation UFSUF−1=SU_F S U_F^{-1} = S fails. After the symmetry-forgetting functor 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤn→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\mathsf{Unit}^{\mathbb{Z}_n}\to\mathsf{Unit} (which drops the symmetry data), η\eta recovers being an isomorphism.

Proof sketch. “⇒\Rightarrow”: If the system is a DTC of order nn, then by 22 there exists an order parameter 𝒪x\mathcal{O}_x whose stroboscopic expectation has period exactly nTnT. Let |ψ⟩|\psi\rangle be a symmetry-broken state in the thermodynamic-limit sense, so ⟨ψ|𝒪x|ψ⟩≠0\langle\psi|\mathcal{O}_x|\psi\rangle\neq 0 but SS permutes the nn symmetry-broken states cyclically. Then UFU_F shifts the order parameter by one cyclic step in this ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n-orbit: UF𝒪xUF−1=ζ𝒪xU_F\mathcal{O}_x U_F^{-1}=\zeta\,\mathcal{O}_x where ζ=e2πik/n\zeta=e^{2\pi i k/n} for some kk coprime to nn (in the symmetry-broken sector). Equivalently, UFSUF−1≠SU_F S U_F^{-1}\neq S: the drive maps SS to UFSUF−1U_F S U_F^{-1}, which is a conjugate of SS by UFU_F (and not equal to SS itself), so the equivariance square fails. This is exactly the statement that η\eta fails to be an isomorphism in 𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍ℤn\mathsf{Unit}^{\mathbb{Z}_n}. Forgetting the symmetry, η\eta becomes the bare unitary UFnU_F^n, which is an isomorphism.

“⇐\Leftarrow”: If UFSUF−1≠SU_F S U_F^{-1}\neq S but UFnSUF−n=SU_F^n S U_F^{-n}=S (which holds because conjugation by UFU_F defines an action of ℤ\mathbb{Z} on the symmetry generator that must factor through ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n by finiteness of the symmetry data), then there is a non-trivial cyclic action of ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n on SS by conjugation by UFU_F. Diagonalising UFU_F in the simultaneous eigenbasis of UFnU_F^n shows that UFU_F has eigenvalues ζke−iε\zeta_k\,e^{-i\varepsilon} with ζk\zeta_k ranging over the nnth roots of unity within each UFnU_F^n-eigenspace. Picking an eigenvector and an order parameter operator overlapping multiple ζk\zeta_k-sectors yields period-nTnT oscillation of the expectation value, proving the system is a DTC of order nn. ◻

25 reformulates the physics of DTCs as an obstruction theory: a DTC is the obstruction to the relation [UF,S]=0[U_F,S]=0 holding strictly. The symmetry-forgetting functor witnesses that the obstruction is not visible to the bare stroboscopic dynamics; it lives in the ℤn\mathbb{Z}_n-equivariant enrichment.

Period-doubling and ℤ2\mathbb{Z}_2 DTCs

The simplest case is n=2n=2. The DTC obstruction class then lives in the ℤ2\mathbb{Z}_2-graded part of End(UF)\mathrm{End}(U_F) that anticommutes with the drive symmetry. We will see in 8 that the kicked Ising chain produces exactly this structure: UFU_F has eigenvalues ±e−iε\pm e^{-i\varepsilon} paired by the global Ising symmetry, giving period-2T2T oscillation of ⟨σiz⟩\langle\sigma^z_i\rangle.

Stability and MBL

The Else–Bauer–Nayak theorem  guarantees stability of the DTC under generic perturbations preserving TT-periodicity, provided the system is many-body localised (MBL). MBL is the obstruction to thermalisation in disordered interacting systems and protects the symmetry-broken sector from heating to infinite temperature. Recent reviews  survey continuous time crystals and DTC variants in clean systems via prethermal mechanisms.

For a spatially periodic system on a dd-dimensional lattice, the static Bloch theorem decomposes the Hamiltonian into a family {H(𝒌)}𝒌∈BZ\{H(\mathbf{k})\}_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathrm{BZ}} on a finite-dimensional fibre. Adding TT-periodic time dependence gives a family H(𝒌,t)H(\mathbf{k},t) doubly periodic in (𝒌,t)∈BZ×S1(\mathbf{k},t)\in\mathrm{BZ}\times S^1.

Definition 26 (Floquet–Bloch evolution). The Floquet–Bloch unitary is the family U(𝒌,t):=𝒯exp⁡(−i∫0tH(𝒌,s)ds),\begin{equation*} U(\mathbf{k},t):=\mathcal{T}\exp\biggl(-i\!\int_0^t H(\mathbf{k},s)\,ds\biggr), \end{equation*} viewed as a smooth map U:BZ×[0,T]→U(N)U\colon\mathrm{BZ}\times[0,T]\to\mathrm{U}(N) with U(𝒌,0)=𝟏U(\mathbf{k},0)=\mathbf{1}.

Categorical reformulation

Functorially, the Floquet–Bloch evolution is a functor BZ×𝑩S1→𝖴𝗇𝗂𝗍\mathrm{BZ}\times \mathbf{B}S^1\to\mathsf{Unit} where BZ\mathrm{BZ} is viewed as the discrete symmetric monoidal groupoid of crystal momenta. Prethermalisation and Heating

Prethermal time scale

Theorem 33 (Abanin–De Roeck–Ho–Huveneers ). Let H(t)H(t) be a TT-periodic, locally-bounded Hamiltonian on a lattice with local energy scale JJ. There exist constants C,c>0C,c>0 depending only on the lattice geometry such that, for ω>CJ\omega>CJ, there is a quasi-local effective Hamiltonian HeffH_{\mathrm{eff}} with ∥U(nT)−e−iHeffnT∥≤CnJe−cω/JfornT≤τ*=1Jecω/J.\begin{equation} \bigl\|U(nT)-e^{-iH_{\mathrm{eff}}\,nT}\bigr\| \leq C n J\,e^{-c\omega/J}\qquad\text{for}\quad nT\leq\tau^*=\frac{1}{J}\,e^{c\omega/J}. \end{equation}

In words: the stroboscopic dynamics is approximated by a static effective Hamiltonian for an exponentially long time scale.

Three regimes

The Floquet dynamics on a generic interacting lattice has three regimes:

  1. Initial transient, t≲Tt\lesssim T: full H(t)H(t) matters; Magnus expansion not yet an approximation.

  2. Prethermal, T≲t≲τ*T\lesssim t\lesssim\tau^*: dynamics governed by Heff(≤N*)H_{\mathrm{eff}}^{(\leq N^*)}. Here 21 applies and Floquet phases are equilibrium phases of HeffH_{\mathrm{eff}}.

  3. Heating, t≳τ*t\gtrsim\tau^*: system absorbs energy from the drive, eventually reaching infinite temperature in a generic interacting bulk .

MBL evades heating

Many-body localised (MBL) systems do not thermalise: emergent local integrals of motion forbid energy absorption beyond a finite bandwidth. Consequently, MBL Floquet systems can support stable (rather than just prethermal) DTC and SPT phases. This is the rigorous content of the Else–Bauer–Nayak theorem.

Remark 34. The prethermal-vs-MBL distinction is reflected in the categorical framework: prethermal phases factor through 21, while MBL Floquet phases do not (the diagram fails to close even on an exponentially long time scale; the obstruction class is non-trivial). This is the precise sense in which DTCs and AFIs are genuinely non-equilibrium phases.

The static SSH model has only the ε=0\varepsilon=0 edge mode (when topological); the ε=π\varepsilon=\pi edge mode is genuinely Floquet — it has no equilibrium counterpart and is detected only by the Floquet winding number, not by any time-averaged invariant.

Open Problems

  1. Continuous time crystals. Spontaneous breaking of continuous time-translation symmetry in a closed quantum system is forbidden by Watanabe–Oshikawa  for the ground state, but variations under drive or in dissipative settings remain open .

  2. Floquet phases in (3+1)(3+1)D. Extending the Roy–Harper periodic table  to interacting systems in 3+13+1 dimensions, including possible non-Abelian Floquet phases.

  3. Floquet many-body scars. Periodic drive stabilising scar states in non-integrable models; relationship between scars and DTC order.

  4. Categorical Floquet RG. A renormalisation-group fixed-point structure on 𝖧𝖺𝗆T\mathsf{Ham}_T, in particular the existence of Floquet conformal field theories as fixed points of the temporal periodicity.

  5. Composition with Law IV. What is the holographic dual of a Floquet CFT? Does the AdS bulk become time-dependent, or does it acquire a temporal S1S^1 factor (giving an AdS-cylindrical bulk)? Initial work  suggests both.

  6. Beyond stroboscopic equivalence. The categorical equivalence relation on 𝖧𝖺𝗆T\mathsf{Ham}_T that captures Floquet phases is broader than stroboscopic equivalence (which fixes UFU_F up to similarity) but narrower than micromotion-blind equivalence; identifying the correct intermediate equivalence is open.
