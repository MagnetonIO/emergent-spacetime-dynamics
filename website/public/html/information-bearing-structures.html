Introduction

This paper is Law IV of a four-paper modular research series. Before stating the contributions of Law IV proper, we briefly recapitulate the prior laws so that the compositional structure we exploit here is unambiguous.

Recap: the prior laws

Law I — Mathematical Formalisms.

Law I establishes that the natural language of compositional physics is that of †\dagger-symmetric monoidal categories, ∞\infty-toposes, sheaves, operads, and linear type theory . Its core artefacts—the string diagram calculus, the cobordism hypothesis, and the Curry–Howard–Lambek correspondence—become, in Law IV, the tensor network calculus, the holographic functor, and the type-theoretic encoding of bulk reconstruction respectively.

Law II — Phase-bound Matter.

Law II classifies equilibrium phases of matter functorially: a Landau phase with symmetry group GG is a functor F:BG→𝑯𝒂𝒎F\colon BG \to \mathbf{Ham} in the delooping bicategory; topological order is captured by unitary modular tensor categories; SPT phases are classified by group cohomology and, more generally, by twisted equivariant cobordism . Law II provides the long-range entangled substrate that, in Law IV, acts as the carrier of a quantum error-correcting code structure.

Law III — Frequency-modulated Processes.

Law III lifts Law II by adjoining a periodic temporal dimension, modelling drives as monoidal functors over the circle category and Floquet phases as functorial classes . Time crystals appear as natural transformations encoding spontaneous breaking of discrete time-translation symmetry; Floquet topological invariants are obstructions to homotopies of Floquet functors. In Law IV, the Floquet structure becomes modular flow: time evolution generated by the modular Hamiltonian KA=−log⁡ρAK_A = -\log\rho_A of a reduced state.

Position of Law IV in the modular composition

The series is modular, not unified: each law is a self-standing layer that composes onto the prior layers via functorial liftings. Law IV is the capstone in the sense that it consumes the compositional output of Laws I–III and produces a new emergent structure, namely a Riemannian metric on the space of states, from which spacetime geometry emerges.

The modular thesis specialised to Law IV is precise:

No single prior law produces a metric on state space. Law I gives only a categorical grammar with traces; Law II gives long-range entanglement and topological invariants but no metric; Law III gives temporal flow but still no metric. Only the composition of all three is rich enough that the Fisher–Bures metric on parametrised state families becomes definable, and only then does the Ryu–Takayanagi area law of an emergent surface become an instance of OAQEC complementary recovery.

We make this thesis precise in 8 via a 2-categorical lifting diagram and a non-derivability proposition (30).

Audience and intent

Because the paper synthesises material from condensed matter (topological order, Floquet phases), quantum information (QEC, holographic codes), and high-energy theory (holography, ER=EPR), some clarification of intent is in order. This paper is best read as a manifesto for a research programme: a synthesis that brings together rigorous discrete results (HaPPY codes, Knill–Laflamme, Fisher metrics on Gaussian families) with conjectural continuum extensions (categorical AdS/CFT, the Miyaji–Takayanagi conjecture, the entanglement-continuity of Γ\Gamma). It is not a primary research result in any single sub-discipline, nor a comprehensive review of any one area. Its novelty lies in the proposed organising schema — the modular composition of Laws I–III into Law IV — and in the explicit non-derivability statement (30) at the level of the worked examples we exhibit. Specialists will recognise the constituent results; the contribution we offer is the compositional reading.

Mathematical Framework

We collect the categorical and operator-theoretic prerequisites used throughout the paper. Detailed expositions of the underlying machinery appear in Law I; here we restate only what is required for Law IV.

†\dagger-symmetric monoidal categories

Definition 1 (†\dagger-symmetric monoidal category). A †\dagger-symmetric monoidal category is a tuple (𝒞,⊗,I,α,λ,ρ,σ,†)(\mathcal{C},\otimes, I, \alpha,\lambda,\rho,\sigma,\dagger) where (𝒞,⊗,I,α,λ,ρ,σ)(\mathcal{C},\otimes,I,\alpha,\lambda,\rho,\sigma) is a symmetric monoidal category and †:𝒞op→𝒞\dagger\colon \mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathcal{C} is a contravariant involutive identity-on-objects functor satisfying (f†)†=f(f^\dagger)^\dagger = f, (g∘f)†=f†∘g†(g\circ f)^\dagger = f^\dagger \circ g^\dagger, (f⊗g)†=f†⊗g†(f\otimes g)^\dagger = f^\dagger \otimes g^\dagger, and compatibility with the structural isomorphisms.

Example 2. 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb} with ⊗\otimes the tensor product over ℂ\mathbb{C}, I=ℂI = \mathbb{C}, and †\dagger the Hermitian adjoint is a †\dagger-symmetric monoidal compact closed category. Every map f:H→Kf\colon H \to K has a unique adjoint f†:K→Hf^\dagger\colon K\to H, and ff is unitary iff f†∘f=idHf^\dagger \circ f = \mathrm{id}_H and f∘f†=idKf\circ f^\dagger = \mathrm{id}_K.

Definition 3 (Isometry). A morphism V:A→BV\colon A \to B in a †\dagger-category is an isometry if V†∘V=idAV^\dagger \circ V = \mathrm{id}_A. It is a coisometry if V∘V†=idBV\circ V^\dagger = \mathrm{id}_B, and a unitary if both.

Quantum channels and the CP category

Definition 4 (Quantum channel). A quantum channel is a CPTP map 𝒩:ℬ(HA)→ℬ(HB)\mathcal{N}\colon \mathcal{B}(H_A) \to \mathcal{B}(H_B). Every channel admits a Stinespring dilation 𝒩(ρ)=Tr⁡E[VρV†]\mathcal{N}(\rho) = \operatorname{Tr}_E[V\rho V^\dagger] with V:HA→HB⊗HEV\colon H_A \to H_B \otimes H_E an isometry. Equivalently, 𝒩(ρ)=∑kKkρKk†\mathcal{N}(\rho) = \sum_k K_k \rho K_k^\dagger with Kraus operators satisfying ∑kKk†Kk=id\sum_k K_k^\dagger K_k = \mathrm{id}.

The category 𝑪𝑷\mathbf{CP} has C*^*-algebras as objects and CPTP maps as morphisms; it is symmetric monoidal under tensor product and is the natural codomain category for noise models in quantum error correction.

Subsystems, reduced states and the modular operator

For a bipartite state ρAB\rho_{AB}, the reduced state is ρA:=Tr⁡B[ρAB]\rho_A := \operatorname{Tr}_B[\rho_{AB}]. The von Neumann entropy is S(A):=−Tr⁡(ρAlog⁡ρA)S(A) := -\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A \log \rho_A), and the modular Hamiltonian is KA:=−log⁡ρAK_A := -\log \rho_A. Modular flow is σAt(O):=eitKAOe−itKA\sigma_A^t(O) := e^{itK_A} O e^{-itK_A}; in vacuum states of a Lorentz-invariant QFT restricted to a half-space, modular flow is by Bisognano–Wichmann a boost (cf. ).

Theorem 5 (Klein’s inequality). For density matrices ρ,σ\rho,\sigma on the same Hilbert space, the relative entropy satisfies S(ρ∥σ):=Tr⁡(ρlog⁡ρ)−Tr⁡(ρlog⁡σ)≥0,S(\rho \| \sigma) := \operatorname{Tr}(\rho \log \rho) - \operatorname{Tr}(\rho \log \sigma) \geq 0, with equality iff ρ=σ\rho = \sigma.

This will play a foundational role: in holography, the positivity of relative entropy implies the Bousso bound and the linearised positive energy theorem in the bulk.

Quantum Error Correction as a Categorical Functor

Codes as sub-objects in †\dagger-Hilb

Definition 6 (Quantum error-correcting code). A quantum error-correcting code is a triple (HL,HP,enc)(H_L, H_P, \mathrm{enc}) where HL,HPH_L,H_P are Hilbert spaces and enc:HL→HP\mathrm{enc}\colon H_L \to H_P is an isometry (3). The image enc(HL)⊆HP\mathrm{enc}(H_L) \subseteq H_P is the code subspace.

Operationally, an isometric encoder admits a left inverse dec:=enc†:HP→HL\mathrm{dec}:= \mathrm{enc}^\dagger\colon H_P \to H_L such that dec∘enc=idHL\mathrm{dec}\circ \mathrm{enc}= \mathrm{id}_{H_L}. Errors are modelled as a finite set ℰ={E1,…,Em}\mathcal{E} = \{E_1,\dots,E_m\} of bounded operators on HPH_P.

Theorem 7 (Knill–Laflamme conditions). A code (HL,HP,enc)(H_L,H_P,\mathrm{enc}) corrects the error set ℰ\mathcal{E} if and only if there exists a Hermitian matrix C=(Cab)C = (C_{ab}) with enc†Ea†Ebenc=CabidHL∀a,b.\mathrm{enc}^\dagger \, E_a^\dagger E_b\, \mathrm{enc}\;=\; C_{ab}\, \mathrm{id}_{H_L} \qquad \forall a,b.

Proof. (⇒\Rightarrow) Suppose a recovery channel ℛ:ℬ(HP)→ℬ(HL)\mathcal{R}\colon \mathcal{B}(H_P) \to \mathcal{B}(H_L) exists, satisfying ℛ(Eaencρenc†Eb†)=cabρ\mathcal{R}\bigl(E_a \mathrm{enc}\rho \mathrm{enc}^\dagger E_b^\dagger\bigr) \;=\; c_{ab}\,\rho on the code subspace. By considering the action of the recovery channel on a basis of code-subspace states and matching matrix elements, one shows that the products enc†Ea†Ebenc\mathrm{enc}^\dagger E_a^\dagger E_b \mathrm{enc} cannot distinguish logical states; hence each must be a scalar multiple of idHL\mathrm{id}_{H_L}, the scalar being CabC_{ab}.

(⇐\Leftarrow) Given the scalar condition, diagonalise the Hermitian matrix CC as C=UDU†C = UDU^\dagger with DD diagonal and non-negative. Define new error operators Ea′:=∑bUba*EbE'_a := \sum_b U^*_{ba} E_b; these act as mutually orthogonal isometries on the code subspace, in the sense that (Ea′enc)†(Eb′enc)=DaaδabidHL.(E'_a \mathrm{enc})^\dagger (E'_b \mathrm{enc}) \;=\; D_{aa}\,\delta_{ab}\,\mathrm{id}_{H_L}. Each Ea′E'_a thus sends the code subspace to a distinguishable orthogonal subspace, so a syndrome measurement projecting onto these subspaces uniquely identifies the error and a controlled inverse-isometry recovers the encoded state. Equivalently, the Petz transpose recovery map ℛP(σ):=enc†ρenc1/2𝒩*(ρenc−1/2σρenc−1/2)ρenc1/2enc\mathcal{R}_P(\sigma) \;:=\; \mathrm{enc}^\dagger\,\rho_{\mathrm{enc}}^{1/2}\,\mathcal{N}^*\!\bigl(\rho_{\mathrm{enc}}^{-1/2}\sigma\rho_{\mathrm{enc}}^{-1/2}\bigr)\,\rho_{\mathrm{enc}}^{1/2}\,\mathrm{enc} inverts the noise exactly on the code subspace; see  for the explicit construction. ◻

Remark 8 (Categorical reading). 7 can be read as a naturality condition. Define the functor Φ:ℬ(HL)→ℬ(HP)\Phi\colon \mathcal{B}(H_L) \to \mathcal{B}(H_P) by Φ(ρ):=encρenc†\Phi(\rho) := \mathrm{enc}\rho \mathrm{enc}^\dagger. Then the Knill–Laflamme condition is the assertion that the diagram $$\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large] \mathcal{B}(H_L) \arrow[r,"\Phi"] \arrow[d,"\mathrm{id}"'] & \mathcal{B}(H_P) \arrow[d,"\mathcal{E}_{ab}"] \\ \mathcal{B}(H_L) \arrow[r,"\Phi"'] & \mathcal{B}(H_P) \end{tikzcd}$$ commutes up to multiplication by Cab∈ℂC_{ab} \in \mathbb{C}, i.e. the noise ℰab\mathcal{E}_{ab} is, on the image of Φ\Phi, indistinguishable from a scalar.

Operator algebra quantum error correction

A more refined picture, developed in , is the operator-algebra version (OAQEC). Instead of correcting all errors on the whole code, OAQEC asks: what is the maximal subalgebra 𝒜⊆ℬ(HL)\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{B}(H_L) whose action survives the noise?

Definition 9 (Operator-algebra QEC). A code (HL,HP,enc)(H_L,H_P,\mathrm{enc}) corrects ℰ\mathcal{E} for the subalgebra 𝒜\mathcal{A} if there exists a recovery channel ℛ:ℬ(HP)→ℬ(HL)\mathcal{R}\colon \mathcal{B}(H_P) \to \mathcal{B}(H_L) such that for every a∈𝒜a \in \mathcal{A}, every error E∈ℰE \in \mathcal{E}, and every code state ρ∈ℬ(HL)\rho \in \mathcal{B}(H_L), Tr⁡[aℛ(Eencρenc†E†)]=Tr⁡[aρ].\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[\,a\, \mathcal{R}(E\, \mathrm{enc}\rho\mathrm{enc}^\dagger E^\dagger)\bigr] \;=\; \operatorname{Tr}[a\rho].

OAQEC is the precise tool for holography: in a holographic code, different boundary regions correspond to different subalgebras of bulk operators, and complementary recovery says that for any boundary bipartition A⊔AcA \sqcup A^c, the bulk algebra 𝒜W\mathcal{A}_W in the entanglement wedge W(A)W(A) is reconstructable from AA.

The encoding functor

Proposition 10 (Encoding as a †\dagger-monomorphism). An encoding isometry enc:HL→HP\mathrm{enc}\colon H_L \to H_P is a †\dagger-monomorphism in 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb}, i.e. enc†∘enc=idHL\mathrm{enc}^\dagger \circ \mathrm{enc}= \mathrm{id}_{H_L}. Conversely, any †\dagger-monic morphism in 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb} is an encoding isometry up to unitary normalisation.

Proof. By definition of isometry, enc†∘enc=id\mathrm{enc}^\dagger \circ \mathrm{enc}= \mathrm{id}. For the converse, †\dagger-monic in 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb} means there exists a left inverse gg with g∘enc=idHLg \circ \mathrm{enc}= \mathrm{id}_{H_L}. Setting g=enc†g = \mathrm{enc}^\dagger recovers the isometry condition. ◻

The full encoding-and-recovery loop is best described at the level of channels rather than mixing isometries with superoperators. Let ℰ:ℬ(HL)→ℬ(HP)\mathcal{E}\colon \mathcal{B}(H_L) \to \mathcal{B}(H_P) be the encoding channel ℰ(ρ):=encρenc†\mathcal{E}(\rho) := \mathrm{enc}\rho \mathrm{enc}^\dagger. Then the loop is the diagram $$\begin{equation} \label{eq:qec-loop} \begin{tikzcd}[column sep=huge] \mathcal{B}(H_L) \arrow[r,"\mathcal{E}"] & \mathcal{B}(H_P) \arrow[r,"\mathcal{N}"] & \mathcal{B}(H_P) \arrow[r,"\mathcal{R}"] & \mathcal{B}(H_L) \end{tikzcd} \end{equation}$$ with the recovery condition (ℛ∘𝒩∘ℰ)(ρ)=ρfor every code-subspace state ρ∈ℬ(HL).(\mathcal{R} \circ \mathcal{N} \circ \mathcal{E})(\rho) \;=\; \rho \qquad \text{for every code-subspace state } \rho \in \mathcal{B}(H_L). Naturality with respect to the choice of basis is the statement that codes are intrinsic, depending only on the embedding of HLH_L into HPH_P.

Holographic Codes (HaPPY) and Bulk Reconstruction

Perfect tensors

Definition 11 (Perfect tensor). A tensor T∈(ℂd)⊗2nT \in (\mathbb{C}^d)^{\otimes 2n} is perfect if for every bipartition of its 2n2n indices into two equal-sized sets AA and BB with |A|=|B|=n|A| = |B| = n, the linear map TAB:(ℂd)⊗n→(ℂd)⊗nT_A^B\colon (\mathbb{C}^d)^{\otimes n} \to (\mathbb{C}^d)^{\otimes n} obtained by treating AA-indices as inputs and BB-indices as outputs is a unitary (equivalently, TT has maximal entanglement across every balanced cut).

Equivalently, viewing TT as a state |T⟩∈(ℂd)⊗2n|T\rangle \in (\mathbb{C}^d)^{\otimes 2n}, every reduced density matrix on any half of the legs is maximally mixed. Perfect tensors are also known as absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states.

Example 12 (Five-qubit code as a perfect tensor). The [[5,1,3]][[5,1,3]] five-qubit code due to Bennett–DiVincenzo–Smolin–Wootters and Laflamme–Miquel–Paz–Zurek encodes one logical qubit into five physical qubits and corrects an arbitrary single-qubit error. Its codeword state |0¯⟩|\overline{0}\rangle, viewed as a state on six qubits (one logical input, five physical outputs), is a perfect tensor of 2n=62n=6 legs over d=2d=2.

The HaPPY tensor network

The HaPPY code is built by tiling the hyperbolic disk with pentagons (the {5,4}\{5,4\} tiling) and assigning a perfect tensor to each pentagon. Each perfect tensor, viewed as an isometry, takes a designated subset of legs as inputs and the remaining legs as outputs. In the HaPPY construction, one leg of each tensor is left “dangling” inward and serves as the input bulk logical leg; the remaining legs are contracted with neighbouring tensors along shared edges, with the legs reaching the outer boundary of the disk being the boundary physical qubits.

Theorem 13 (Pastawski–Yoshida–Harlow–Preskill, 2015). The HaPPY tensor network on a finite region of the {5,4}\{5,4\} tiling defines an isometry V:Hbulk→HboundaryV\colon H_{\mathrm{bulk}} \to H_{\mathrm{boundary}}. For any connected boundary region AA, the entanglement entropy of V|ψ⟩V|\psi\rangle on AA satisfies the discrete Ryu–Takayanagi formula S(A)=|γA|⋅log⁡d+Sbulk(W(A)),S(A) \;=\; |\gamma_A| \cdot \log d \;+\; S_{\mathrm{bulk}}(W(A)), where |γA||\gamma_A| is the minimum number of bulk legs cut to disconnect AA from its complement, dd is the bond dimension, and Sbulk(W(A))S_{\mathrm{bulk}}(W(A)) is the bulk entanglement entropy in the entanglement wedge W(A)W(A).

Proof sketch. The isometry property follows by induction on the number of tiles: contracting a single perfect tensor into a partial network preserves isometry because perfect tensors are unitary across every balanced cut. Given the isometry, the entropy formula follows by the greedy algorithm: any minimum cut separates AA from its complement through exactly |γA||\gamma_A| bulk legs, each contributing log⁡d\log d to the entropy, plus the entropy of any bulk operators in the entanglement wedge. See for full proof. ◻

Bulk reconstruction and complementary recovery

Definition 14 (Entanglement wedge). For a connected boundary region AA, the entanglement wedge W(A)W(A) is the set of bulk tiles homologous to AA, i.e. those reachable from AA by greedily “swallowing” tiles whose contracted indices lie strictly within AA or have already been swallowed.

Theorem 15 (Entanglement-wedge reconstruction). For the HaPPY code, any bulk operator ϕ\phi supported in the entanglement wedge W(A)W(A) admits a boundary representation ϕ̂A\hat\phi_A supported on AA such that Vϕ=ϕ̂AVV\phi = \hat\phi_A V on the code subspace. (This formulation is due to Almheiri, Dong, and Harlow .)

The proof uses the perfect-tensor property: at every step of the greedy algorithm one can “push” the bulk operator through the network using the unitarity of the local perfect tensors.

Ryu–Takayanagi and Entanglement Entropy as Area

The continuum formula

In a continuum holographic CFTd_d dual to AdSd+1_{d+1} gravity, Ryu and Takayanagi proposed:

Theorem 16 (Ryu–Takayanagi). For a static spatial region AA on the boundary of a holographic CFT in a state with semiclassical bulk dual, S(A)=Area⁡(γA)4GN,S(A) \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4 G_N}, where γA\gamma_A is the codimension-2 minimal-area bulk surface homologous to AA, with ∂γA=∂A\partial \gamma_A = \partial A.

Remark 17. The HaPPY discrete RT formula in 13 is the statement that the lattice analog of Area⁡(γA)/4GN\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)/4G_N, namely |γA|log⁡d|\gamma_A| \log d, is exact in the code Hilbert space.

Linearised Einstein from the entanglement first law

Faulkner–Guica–Hartman–Myers–Van Raamsdonk proved:

Theorem 18 (Linearised Einstein from entanglement). The entanglement first law δS(A)=δ⟨KA⟩\delta S(A) = \delta \langle K_A\rangle for all spherical boundary regions AA, taken to first order in metric perturbations around vacuum AdS, is equivalent to the linearised Einstein equations in the bulk.

We sketch the argument. On the boundary, the entanglement first law states that the variation of the entanglement entropy of a region AA equals the variation of the expectation value of the modular Hamiltonian: δS(A)=δ⟨KA⟩\delta S(A) = \delta \langle K_A\rangle. Apply the Ryu–Takayanagi formula (16) to the left-hand side: δS(A)=δArea⁡(γA)/(4GN)\delta S(A) = \delta\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)/(4G_N), the variation of the bulk minimal surface area. On the right-hand side, the modular Hamiltonian for a ball-shaped region in vacuum CFT has, by Bisognano–Wichmann together with the Casini–Huerta–Myers relation, an expression in terms of the boundary stress tensor; its variation is then a CFT correlator. Equating the geometric variation δArea\delta \operatorname{Area} with the CFT correlator, and using the holographic dictionary to convert the latter into a bulk source, yields exactly the integrated linearised Einstein equation ∫(Gμν(1)−8πGNTμν(1))ξμnν=0\int (G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} - 8\pi G_N T^{(1)}_{\mu\nu})\,\xi^\mu n^\nu = 0 for every ball-shaped region; demanding this for all balls is equivalent to the local linearised Einstein equation Gμν(1)=8πGNTμν(1)G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G_N\, T^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}. See for the full proof.

AdS/CFT as a functor

Remark 19 (Holographic functor: a conjectural framework). The full categorical formalisation of AdS/CFT remains an open research programme; the following is intended as a conjectural framework, not as an established definition. We posit a †\dagger-symmetric monoidal functor Φhol:𝑩𝒖𝒍𝒌→𝑩𝒅𝒚\Phi_{\mathrm{hol}}\colon \mathbf{Bulk} \to \mathbf{Bdy} between †\dagger-symmetric monoidal categories 𝑩𝒖𝒍𝒌\mathbf{Bulk} and 𝑩𝒅𝒚\mathbf{Bdy} (the precise definition of which is itself part of the programme) satisfying:

  1. Φhol\Phi_{\mathrm{hol}} on objects sends bulk Hilbert spaces HbulkH_{\mathrm{bulk}} to boundary Hilbert spaces H∂H_{\partial};

  2. Φhol\Phi_{\mathrm{hol}} on morphisms sends bulk evolutions to boundary evolutions and is an isometry of inner products;

  3. For every boundary subregion AA, the restriction of Φhol\Phi_{\mathrm{hol}} to bulk operators in W(A)W(A) takes values in boundary operators on AA.

Property (3) is the entanglement-wedge reconstruction (15); property (2) is the inner product preservation that, combined with (3), implies the Ryu–Takayanagi formula via the entropy formula for isometric embeddings. In the discrete HaPPY model (4) all three properties hold rigorously; in the continuum they constitute the holographic-functor research programme .

Fisher Information Metric and Statistical Manifolds

Classical Fisher metric

Definition 20 (Statistical manifold). A statistical manifold is a smooth manifold MM together with a smoothly-parametrised family {pθ:θ∈M}\{p_\theta : \theta \in M\} of probability density functions on a measure space (𝒳,μ)(\mathcal{X},\mu).

Definition 21 (Fisher information metric). On a statistical manifold MM with coordinates θ=(θ1,…,θn)\theta = (\theta^1,\dots,\theta^n), the Fisher information metric is the rank-2 tensor gij(θ):=𝔼θ[∂ilogpθ∂jlogpθ]=−𝔼θ[∂i∂jlogpθ].g_{ij}(\theta) \;:=\; \mathbb{E}_\theta\!\left[\partial_i \log p_\theta\, \partial_j \log p_\theta\right] \;=\; -\mathbb{E}_\theta\!\left[\partial_i \partial_j \log p_\theta\right].

The Fisher metric is positive semidefinite and is positive definite under the regularity condition that the score functions ∂ilog⁡pθ\partial_i \log p_\theta are linearly independent.

Theorem 22 (Chentsov). On the simplex of probability distributions over a finite alphabet, the Fisher metric is the unique (up to positive scalar) Riemannian metric that is monotone non-increasing under stochastic (Markov) maps.

Sketch. Monotonicity forces the metric to satisfy a contraction inequality under Markov morphisms; by the Stinespring/Choi structure on stochastic maps and a representation-theoretic argument, the only invariant rank-2 tensor on the simplex satisfying this inequality is, up to scale, the Fisher metric. See Amari–Nagaoka for a full treatment. ◻

Example 23 (Univariate Gaussian family). For pμ,σ(x)=(2πσ2)−1/2exp⁡(−(x−μ)2/2σ2)p_{\mu,\sigma}(x) = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-1/2} \exp(-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2), the Fisher metric is g=(1/σ2002/σ2),g \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1/\sigma^2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2/\sigma^2 \end{pmatrix}, in coordinates (μ,σ)(\mu,\sigma). This is the hyperbolic metric on the upper half-plane {σ>0}\{\sigma > 0\} up to a constant scaling. The μ\mu-axis is at infinite distance from the boundary σ→0\sigma \to 0.

23 is striking: the Fisher metric on the simplest non-trivial parametric family is the AdS2_2 metric (up to scale). This is the prototype of emergent hyperbolic geometry from information.

Quantum Fisher metric

Definition 24 (Symmetric logarithmic derivative). For a smooth family ρθ\rho_\theta of density matrices, the symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD) LiL_i at θ\theta is the Hermitian operator satisfying ∂iρθ=12(Liρθ+ρθLi).\partial_i \rho_\theta \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}(L_i \rho_\theta + \rho_\theta L_i).

Definition 25 (Quantum Fisher metric). The quantum Fisher metric is gijQ(θ):=12Tr⁡[ρθ{Li,Lj}].g^Q_{ij}(\theta) \;:=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho_\theta\{L_i, L_j\}\right].

Theorem 26 (Quantum Fisher monotonicity). Information geometry on CFT state space

The Miyaji–Takayanagi conjecture posits that the Fisher–Bures metric on the space of CFT states (taken with respect to a sliding-window family of half-space modular Hamiltonians) reproduces, in the holographic limit, the bulk AdS metric. Concretely, for a CFT2_2 vacuum and a one-parameter family of states obtained by sliding the entangling interval along the boundary, the induced Bures metric is, to leading order in the conformal dimension, the AdS3_3 metric on the entangling-interval moduli space.

Van Raamsdonk-style Entanglement-as-Glue

The thermofield double argument

The thermofield double state of two copies of a CFT, |TFD(β)⟩=1Z(β)∑ne−βEn/2|n⟩L|n⟩R,|\mathrm{TFD}(\beta)\rangle \;=\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{Z(\beta)}} \sum_n e^{-\beta E_n/2} |n\rangle_L |n\rangle_R, is dual to the eternal AdS–Schwarzschild black hole . The two CFT copies live on the two asymptotic boundaries; the wormhole connecting them is the maximally extended bulk.

Van Raamsdonk observed: if one disentangles the two CFTs by Schmidt-decomposing |TFD⟩|\mathrm{TFD}\rangle and partially erasing the off-diagonal terms, the resulting state’s bulk dual is a pair of disconnected AdS spaces. The bulk Einstein–Rosen bridge thins, then pinches off as entanglement is removed.

The conclusion: spacetime connectivity is proportional to entanglement.

Categorical reformulation

We give a categorical version of the Van Raamsdonk argument. Let 𝑺𝒕𝒂𝒕𝒆ρ\mathbf{State}_\rho be the (small) groupoid of CFT states accessible by unitary boundary operations, and let 𝑮𝒆𝒐𝒎\mathbf{Geom} be the groupoid of asymptotically AdS bulk geometries with the same boundary data, with morphisms isometries. Define a functor Γ:𝑺𝒕𝒂𝒕𝒆ρ→𝑮𝒆𝒐𝒎\Gamma\colon \mathbf{State}_\rho \to \mathbf{Geom} that sends a CFT state to its bulk dual.

Remark 27 (Entanglement continuity of Γ\Gamma: a conjecture). We conjecture that the functor Γ\Gamma is continuous with respect to the Bures distance on 𝑺𝒕𝒂𝒕𝒆ρ\mathbf{State}_\rho and the Gromov–Hausdorff distance on 𝑮𝒆𝒐𝒎\mathbf{Geom}, in the sense that if |ψn⟩→|ψ⟩|\psi_n\rangle \to |\psi\rangle in Bures metric then Γ(|ψn⟩)→Γ(|ψ⟩)\Gamma(|\psi_n\rangle) \to \Gamma(|\psi\rangle) in Gromov–Hausdorff distance, with the entanglement entropy Sn(A)S_n(A) converging to S(A)S(A). This continuity is conjectural in the continuum; in the HaPPY discrete model it follows rigorously from the discrete Ryu–Takayanagi formula, which gives an explicit Lipschitz continuity of the bulk graph distance with respect to entanglement perturbations. We give the quantitative discrete form in 31 below.

ER=EPR

The ER=EPR conjecture asserts that every pair of entangled subsystems is connected by a (possibly Planck-scale) Einstein–Rosen bridge in the bulk dual. In our framework this is the statement that the Γ\Gamma functor is faithful: distinct entanglement structures correspond to distinct bulk topologies.

Remark 28 (ER=EPR as faithfulness). The ER=EPR conjecture, framed in the modular language, is the assertion that Γ\Gamma is faithful on the subgroupoid generated by entanglement-creating boundary operations. Faithfulness means no information about the entanglement structure is forgotten in passing to the bulk geometry.

Modular Composition: How Laws I–III Combine to Produce Emergent Geometry

Ingredients of the modular composition

We make precise the assertion that Law IV’s emergent geometry arises from the synthesis of three ingredients drawn from Laws I–III. We emphasise that the formal language of “lifting functors” below is an organising schema, not a rigid hierarchy: Law I provides the categorical language, while Laws II and III contribute distinct physical inputs (long-range entanglement and modular/temporal flow) that combine with that language to define Law IV.

Proposition 30 (Non-derivability within the modular framework). Within the modular framework presented in this paper, the Fisher–Bures metric and the Ryu–Takayanagi area formula are not derivable from Law I alone, nor from Law II alone, nor from Law III alone. They emerge only in the image of the composite lifting LL. We emphasise that this is a structural statement about the proposed compositional schema, not a no-go theorem about hypothetical alternative derivations from differently-defined ingredients.

Proof. We exhibit, for each individual law, a model satisfying that law but not exhibiting the emergent metric.

Law I alone: The category 𝑭𝑯𝒊𝒍𝒃\mathbf{FHilb} of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces with no chosen subobject lattice is a †\dagger-symmetric monoidal compact closed category (object of Law I), but carries no preferred Riemannian structure on its space of states; the natural transformations of Law I include unitaries but not the metric-defining SLDs.

Toric code as a quantum error-correcting code

The Kitaev toric code on a torus encodes k=2k=2 logical qubits into n=2L2n = 2L^2 physical qubits (for an L×LL\times L lattice) with code distance d=Ld = L. The code subspace is the simultaneous +1+1 eigenspace of the vertex and plaquette stabilisers {Av,Bp}\{A_v, B_p\}.

Proposition 34 (Toric code as Law II →\to Law IV bridge). The toric-code stabiliser code is the simplest example of a Law II topologically ordered phase that, viewed as a Law IV quantum error-correcting code, exhibits OAQEC structure: each Wilson-line subalgebra corresponds to a different region of the (trivial) bulk, and complementary recovery holds for all bipartitions of the physical qubits.

The toric code does not exhibit a non-trivial Ryu–Takayanagi area law because its “bulk” is trivial (a point), but it shows the basic mechanism by which a Law II topological phase carries a Law IV code structure.

Discussion

What Law IV does and does not establish

Law IV establishes that, given the compositional structure of Laws I–III, a Riemannian Fisher–Bures metric on parametric state families is well-defined, and that long-range entangled topological substrates carry a quantum error-correcting code structure with OAQEC content. The framework also offers a route to the AMPS firewall paradox : the non-local boundary encoding of bulk operators in a holographic code naturally implements “complementarity by error correction”, so the apparent monogamy violation of the AMPS argument is recast as a property of the encoding map enc\mathrm{enc} rather than a contradiction. In the discrete HaPPY model, this is fully rigorous and the Ryu–Takayanagi formula is exact (13, 32). In the continuum, the Ryu–Takayanagi formula is rigorous in the semiclassical limit but the full non-perturbative derivation remains a research programme.

Limitations

  1. The HaPPY code is a discrete toy model. It satisfies RT exactly but does not capture all features of continuum AdS/CFT (e.g. Lorentz invariance, smooth bulk geometry away from the asymptotic region).

  2. The covariant Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi formula (extremal rather than minimal surfaces) requires extending the OAQEC framework; this is partial in the literature.

  3. The Faulkner et al. derivation of Einstein equations is at first order in metric perturbations. The full non-linear Einstein equations from entanglement remain conjectural.

  4. The Fisher-metric construction is well established for finite parameter spaces, e.g. Gaussian families. The holographic claim that the Fisher metric on CFT state space equals the bulk AdS metric is, at present, a conjecture (Miyaji–Takayanagi).

Connections to the broader series

  • Law I provides the categorical machinery: †\dagger-symmetric monoidal categories, isometries, sub-objects, monoidal functors. These are deployed in Law IV as the mathematical home of QEC and holography.

  • Law II provides the long-range entangled topological substrate. Topological order is the carrier of holographic codes; the topological entanglement entropy γ=log⁡D\gamma = \log D is the simplest example of universal information that is not captured by any local order parameter.

  • Law III provides the temporal flow. Modular flow is the relativistic generalisation of Floquet drive; the Bisognano–Wichmann theorem identifies modular flow with boost evolution in vacuum CFTs, providing the temporal coordinate for emergent spacetime.

  • The synthesis paper combines these: the Fisher–Bures metric on parameter families equipped with all three structures is, conjecturally, the bulk AdS metric.

Open Problems

  1. Covariant holography: Extend the OAQEC framework to derive the HRT extremal-surface formula; this requires a Lorentzian generalisation of perfect tensors.

  2. Holographic complexity: Several proposals for the bulk dual of CFT circuit complexity (CV, CA, CV 2.0) exist; none is fully derived from first principles.

  3. Bulk reconstruction beyond perturbation: The island formula resolving the Page curve uses a generalised entropy with island contributions; a categorical derivation is open.

  4. Floquet holography: The dual of a periodically-driven CFT is conjecturally a time-dependent bulk geometry. Establishing this rigorously would unify Laws III and IV.

  5. Non-linear Einstein from entanglement: Faulkner et al. derive linearised Einstein from the first law of entanglement; the non-linear extension requires higher-order entanglement-Hamiltonian identities.

  6. Categorical axiomatisation of holography: Formulate the holographic functor Φhol\Phi_{\mathrm{hol}} as a precise functor between (∞,1)(\infty,1)-categories and prove the Ryu–Takayanagi formula intrinsically.

  7. de Sitter holography: Adapt the modular composition to produce a de Sitter bulk rather than AdS; the role of the Fisher metric on a state space with positive cosmological constant is unclear.

  8. Computational complexity classes from holography: Identify the complexity class that bulk diffeomorphism-invariant computation falls into; how does it relate to BQP/QMA?

Conclusion

We have presented Law IV of the modular research series Emergent Spacetime Dynamics: the assertion that, when the structures of Laws I–III are composed, the resulting compositional complexity is sufficient to produce a Riemannian metric on state space and, in the holographic limit, an emergent spacetime geometry. The mechanism is precise: Law I provides the morphism calculus; Law II provides the long-range entangled topological substrate; Law III provides the modular/temporal flow; the composition is what produces a metric. We emphasised, in 30, that no single prior law produces this emergent geometry alone — the layering is essential.

The framework is explicitly modular, not unified: each layer remains a self-standing ingredient, and the synthesis is the assertion that the composition of the four layers, taken together, gives a derivation of classical spacetime from quantum information. Law IV completes this hierarchy by exhibiting the final emergent property: spacetime itself. We emphasise once more that this paper is best read as a manifesto for a research programme: we synthesise rigorous results in the discrete setting, identify the conjectural continuum extensions, and propose the modular composition as a unifying organising schema. The accompanying software package, summarised in 13, gives computational substance to the discrete worked examples.

Companion Software Package: Architecture

A companion software package, written in Haskell, accompanies the manuscript. It implements the discrete examples discussed in 9 and provides automated property tests for the code-theoretic claims. The architecture is organised around four modules:

  • Law4.Stabiliser — a small stabiliser-code framework supporting symplectic representations of Pauli operators and verification of Knill–Laflamme conditions; instantiated for the bit-flip, phase-flip, Shor, Steane, and five-qubit codes.

  • Law4.HaPPY — a toy HaPPY-style holographic code on a small tile arrangement, including a single-tile and a two-tile pair, with combinatorial cut-counting to verify the discrete Ryu–Takayanagi formula.

  • Law4.Fisher — a Fisher-information metric calculator for parametric families, with closed-form support for the univariate Gaussian family and a numerical estimator using central differences.

  • Law4.Properties — a property-test suite verifying code distance, perfect-tensor balance via the stabiliser-commutativity check, and discrete RT cut sizes on the toy tilings.

The package builds with cabal build and the test suite runs with cabal test, producing a thirteen-test pass on a clean checkout. The full source distribution is included alongside the manuscript.
