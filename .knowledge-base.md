## Executive Overview

This knowledge base scaffolds a four-paper modular research series arguing that the structure of physical reality — from matter phases through spacetime geometry — emerges hierarchically from categorical and informational structures. The framework is explicitly **modular**, not unified: each law provides a self-standing mathematical layer that composes onto prior laws via functorial liftings and natural transformations, producing new emergent properties at each level of composition.

Law I establishes the categorical primitives — monoidal categories, functors, sheaves, topoi, operads, and their type-theoretic encodings — that serve as the shared grammatical substrate. Law II uses these primitives to classify phases of matter (thermodynamic and topological) as functorial classes over symmetry-group categories, showing Landau's paradigm and its topological generalizations as special cases. Law III extends the framework temporally: periodic drive Hamiltonians become monoidal functors over the circle category, Floquet phases arise as new invariants, and time crystals instantiate symmetry breaking in the time direction — all expressed as natural transformations between the functors constructed in Laws I and II. Law IV completes the hierarchy by showing that sufficiently rich compositional structure from Laws I–III produces emergent information geometry: quantum error-correcting codes become holographic codes, the geometry of their tensor networks encodes AdS/CFT duality, and Fisher information metrics provide Riemannian structure on state space from which spacetime itself emerges. The synthesis paper then argues that this four-step modular composition, taken together, is the mechanism by which any sufficiently structured quantum information substrate bootstraps the appearance of classical spacetime.

These definitions are shared across all four laws and should be introduced carefully in Law I and referenced by name thereafter.

## Law I — Mathematical Formalisms

### Scope
Law I provides the categorical grammar that all subsequent laws speak. It is NOT a physics paper per se — it is a mathematical foundations paper establishing the precise language for matter-information correspondence. The central thesis is that the correct mathematical language for physical theories is that of symmetric monoidal (∞-)categories, and that the Hilbert space formalism, statistical mechanics, gauge theory, and type-theoretic computation are all instances of a single categorical semantics.

### Key Concepts

**1. Monoidal Categories and Physics**

A *symmetric monoidal category* (SMC) (C, ⊗, I, α, λ, ρ, σ) consists of:
- Tensor product ⊗: C × C → C
- Unit object I
- Associator α: (A⊗B)⊗C → A⊗(B⊗C)
- Left/right unitors λ: I⊗A → A, ρ: A⊗I → A
- Symmetry σ: A⊗B → B⊗A
satisfying coherence axioms (Mac Lane's pentagon and triangle; symmetry hexagons).

Physical systems compose as tensor products. States of a system A are morphisms I → A (points). Processes are morphisms A → B. Measurement outcomes are morphisms A → I.

The *string diagram calculus* for SMCs provides a 2D topological notation where:
- Objects are wires (strings)
- Morphisms are boxes connecting wires
- Tensor product is juxtaposition (side-by-side)
- Composition is vertical stacking
- Symmetry isomorphism is wire crossing

String diagrams are equivalent to morphism expressions by the coherence theorem (Joyal-Street 1991).

**2. Compact Closed Categories and Quantum Mechanics**

In a compact closed category, every object A has a dual A* with unit η: I → A*⊗A and counit ε: A⊗A* → I. This gives:
- Trace: Tr(f) = ε ∘ (id_{A*}⊗f) ∘ η for f: A → A
- Partial trace (for entangled systems)
- Transpose morphisms (time-reversal)

FHilb (finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, linear maps) is compact closed. The dual of H is H* (conjugate space).

Adding a †-structure (Abramsky-Coecke): a contravariant involutive functor †: C^op → C, identity on objects, satisfying (f†)† = f. In FHilb, f† is the adjoint. †-compact closed categories provide:
- Unitaries: morphisms f where f† ∘ f = id and f ∘ f† = id
- Orthogonal bases: †-special commutative Frobenius algebras (Coecke-Pavlovic-Vicary)
- Born rule: probability p(m|ψ) = |ε ∘ (m†⊗id) ∘ η ∘ ψ|²

**3. Sheaves, Topoi, and Local Observables**

A *sheaf* F on a topological space X (or site C with Grothendieck topology) is a presheaf F: Open(X)^op → Set satisfying:
- Local condition: if {U_i} covers U and s,t ∈ F(U) agree on each U_i, then s = t
- Gluing condition: compatible local sections glue to a unique global section

Physical interpretation: F(U) = observables measurable in region U; gluing = local observables assemble into global ones; gauge fields are sections of associated sheaves.

A *Grothendieck topos* E is a category equivalent to the category of sheaves Sh(C, J) on a site (C, J). Topoi have:
- All finite limits and colimits
- Exponential objects (function spaces)
- A subobject classifier Ω (truth-values object)
- Internal language: intuitionistic higher-order logic

Every Grothendieck topos has an associated internal language (Mitchell-Bénabou language); physics formulated in a topos has a built-in logical/type-theoretic encoding.

**4. Operads and Algebraic Structure**

An operad O specifies multi-input operations:
- O(n) = set of n-ary operations
- Composition maps: O(n) × O(k₁) × ... × O(k_n) → O(k₁+...+k_n)
- Unit: element in O(1)
- Σ_n-equivariance (symmetric operads)

An algebra over O is an assignment of a vector space (or object of C) A and linear maps O(n) ⊗ A^⊗n → A satisfying associativity/unity. Examples:
- Associative operad Ass: algebras are associative algebras
- Commutative operad Com: algebras are commutative algebras
- Lie operad Lie: algebras are Lie algebras
- E_n operads (little n-disks): algebras are n-fold loop spaces; E_∞ = commutative algebras up to all coherent homotopies

Physical application: observables in QFT form an algebra over the E_n operad encoding causal structure (factorization algebras of Costello-Gwilliam).

**5. Type-Theoretic Encoding**

The Curry-Howard-Lambek correspondence:
- Propositions ↔ Types ↔ Objects of a category
- Proofs ↔ Programs ↔ Morphisms
- Normalization ↔ Computation ↔ β-reduction

For quantum mechanics: *Linear* type theory (no contraction, no weakening) encodes:
- Linear types = quantum resources (no-cloning)
- Tensor type A⊗B = joint quantum system
- Linear function type A ⊸ B = quantum channel from A to B
- ! (of-course) modality = classical resource (duplicable)

Quantum circuits as morphisms in a dagger compact category correspond to programs in a linear λ-calculus with dagger. Proto-Quipper-M formalizes this (Rios-Selinger 2017).

Dependent types extend this: quantum circuit families indexed by classical parameters (sizes, angles). Linear dependent type theory (Fu-Kishida-Selinger 2020) provides the language.

In Haskell, this is approximated via:
- Linear types (GHC LinearTypes extension, GHC 9.0+)
- Phantom types encoding dimension/system information
- Typeclasses for monoidal structure (Category, Arrow, SymMonCat)

**6. Categorical Reformulation of Hilbert Space and Statistical Mechanics**

Hilbert space: The category Hilb of Hilbert spaces and bounded linear maps is a †-symmetric monoidal category. The functor L²(−): Meas → Hilb from measurable spaces to Hilbert spaces is monoidal (L²(X×Y) ≅ L²(X) ⊗ L²(Y)).

Statistical mechanics: The category of probability spaces and measurable maps; the functor assigning to a state the density matrix; partition functions as traces. Baez-Fritz-Leinster (2011) give a categorical characterization of Shannon entropy: it is the unique functor FinProb → [0,∞) satisfying functoriality, chain rule, and continuity.

Quantum statistical mechanics: States are density matrices ρ ∈ End(H) with ρ ≥ 0, Tr(ρ) = 1. The category is CP (completely positive maps), which is the Kleisli category of a certain monad on Hilb. The KMS condition for thermal states is a sheaf condition over the real line with the temperature as parameter.

### Key Theorems and Definitions

**Theorem (Mac Lane Coherence, 1963)**: Every diagram in a free monoidal category built from the structural isomorphisms commutes. Consequently, all well-typed rearrangements of tensor products are canonically equal — association order doesn't matter up to canonical isomorphism.

**Theorem (Joyal-Street, 1991)**: For symmetric monoidal categories, string diagrams are a sound and complete calculus: two morphisms in the free SMC are equal if and only if their string diagrams are isotopic as plane graphs.

**Theorem (Cobordism Hypothesis, Baez-Dolan 1995, Lurie 2009)**: The (∞,n)-category Bord_n^{fr} of framed bordisms is the free symmetric monoidal (∞,n)-category with duals generated by a single object (the point). Consequently, extended TQFTs Z: Bord_n^{fr} → C are classified by fully dualizable objects of C.

**Theorem (Drinfeld-Majid)**: The category of representations Rep(G) of a finite group G is a symmetric tensor category with fiber functor to Vect_k. Conversely, every finite symmetric tensor category with a fiber functor is equivalent to Rep(G) for some group G. (Tannaka-Krein duality.)

**Definition (Frobenius Algebra)**: An object A in a monoidal category C with multiplication μ: A⊗A → A, comultiplication δ: A → A⊗A, unit η: I → A, counit ε: A → I, satisfying unit/counit axioms and the Frobenius law: (id⊗δ) ∘ μ = (μ⊗id) ∘ (id⊗δ) ... (and symmetrically). In FHilb, Frobenius algebras correspond to orthonormal bases; †-Frobenius algebras = orthogonal bases with orthogonal projections.

**Definition (Factorization Algebra)**: A factorization algebra F on a manifold M assigns cochain complexes F(U) to open sets U, with multi-linear maps F(U₁) ⊗ ... ⊗ F(U_n) → F(V) for pairwise disjoint Uᵢ ⊂ V, satisfying a homotopy-coherent version of gluing. In a quantum field theory, the observables supported in U form F(U). (Costello-Gwilliam.)

## Law II — Phase-bound Matter

### Scope
Law II uses the language of Law I to classify phases of matter — both thermodynamic (Landau) and topological (Wen) — as functorial classes over appropriate categorical structures. The central thesis is that a phase of matter is a class of ground states stable under perturbations, and this class is precisely the equivalence class of a functor from a symmetry-group category to the category of Hamiltonians, under the equivalence relation of smooth deformation without gap closing. Topological phases go beyond this: they are classified by topological invariants that are natural transformations between such functors.

### Key Concepts

**1. Landau Theory as Functorial Semantics**

Landau's theory of phase transitions: a phase is characterized by a local order parameter ⟨φ⟩ (a section of a G-equivariant vector bundle over configuration space), which vanishes in the symmetric phase and is nonzero in the broken-symmetry phase.

Functorial reformulation: Let G be a symmetry group, Rep(G) the category of representations, and Ham the category of gapped Hamiltonians (with morphisms: adiabatic deformations). Then:
- A Landau phase with symmetry group G and order parameter in representation V is a functor F: BG → Ham, where BG is the delooping of G (one-object category with Hom(*, *) = G).
- A phase transition corresponds to a natural transformation η: F ⇒ F' that is not an isomorphism at the Hamiltonian level (gap closes).
- The free energy functional is the action of F on generating morphisms.

The Ginzburg-Landau free energy density:
```
f[φ] = a(T)|φ|² + b|φ|⁴ + c|∇φ|² + ...
```
is a G-invariant functional of sections of the order parameter bundle. Near the critical point, it governs the universality class (determined by the representation V and the spatial dimension d).

Renormalization group (RG) as a functor: Wilson's RG defines a coarsening functor RG: Ham → Ham that acts on the space of Hamiltonians. Fixed points are RG-invariant Hamiltonians (critical points). RG flows are natural transformations between Hamiltonian functors at different scales.

**2. Topological Order**

Topological order is a phase of matter characterized by ground-state degeneracy that:
- Depends on the genus g of the spatial manifold (e.g., 4^g-fold for Z_2 toric code)
- Is stable under any local perturbation
- Is NOT captured by any local order parameter (goes beyond Landau paradigm)

The algebraic data of a topological phase in (2+1)D is a *unitary modular tensor category* (UMTC) C with:
- Simple objects {1, a, b, ...} = anyon types (with 1 = vacuum)
- Fusion rules: a ⊗ b ≅ ⊕_c N^c_{ab} · c (fusion multiplicities N^c_{ab})
- F-matrices (recoupling/associativity data): F^{abc}_d: ∑_e ∈ N^{ab}_e N^{ec}_d → ∑_f N^{af}_{bc→d} ... (6j-symbols)
- R-matrices (braiding): R^{ab}_c: morphism implementing exchange of a,b in fusion channel c
- Twist θ_a = e^{2πi h_a} (topological spin)
- S-matrix: S_{ab} = Tr(R_{ba} ∘ R_{ab}) / D (non-degenerate = "modular" condition)
- Quantum dimension d_a, total quantum dimension D = √(∑_a d_a²)

The Pentagon equation: F-matrices satisfy the Mac Lane pentagon (associativity coherence). The Hexagon equations: F and R matrices satisfy hexagon coherence (braiding coherence). These equations are the defining constraints of a braided monoidal category.

**3. Kitaev's Quantum Double and Toric Code**

For finite group G, the quantum double D(G) = Fun(G) ⋊ G is a Hopf algebra whose representation category Rep(D(G)) is a UMTC — this is the algebraic content of Kitaev's toric code generalization.

The toric code (G = Z_2):
- Hilbert space: ⊗_{edges} C²
- Hamiltonian: H = -∑_v A_v - ∑_p B_p
  - A_v = ∏_{e∋v} σ^x_e (vertex stabilizer)
  - B_p = ∏_{e∈p} σ^z_e (plaquette stabilizer)
- Ground state: simultaneous +1 eigenstate of all A_v, B_p
- Anyons: e (electric, A_v = -1 violation), m (magnetic, B_p = -1 violation), ε = em (fermion)
- Braiding: e around m acquires phase -1 (semion statistics for ε)

**4. String-Net Condensation (Levin-Wen)**

The Levin-Wen model associates a unitary fusion category (UFC) C to each topological phase:
- To each edge assign an object of C (string label)
- Hamiltonian is a sum of commuting projectors onto string-net states (branching rules of C)
- Ground state is the string-net condensate: superposition of all closed string-net configurations with weights given by the categorical data of C
- Excitations are ends of open strings = anyon types
- Topological order classified by Drinfeld center Z(C): the UMTC of anyons arising from string-net C

Key result: Every (2+1)D topological phase (satisfying reasonable physical assumptions) is equivalent to a Levin-Wen model for some UFC C.

**5. Symmetry-Protected Topological (SPT) Phases**

SPT phases are gapped, short-range entangled (SRE) phases (trivial topological order = no anyons) that are distinct ONLY in the presence of a symmetry G.

Classification (Chen-Gu-Wen 2013): Bosonic SPT phases in d spatial dimensions with on-site symmetry G are labeled by elements of the group cohomology H^{d+1}(G, U(1)).

The group cohomology H^n(G, M) = Z^n(G,M)/B^n(G,M) where:
- n-cocycles: functions ω: G^n → M satisfying the cocycle condition δω = 0
- n-coboundaries: functions of the form δf for f: G^{n-1} → M
- The coboundary map: δω(g₁,...,g_{n+1}) = ω(g₂,...,g_{n+1}) · ∏_{i=1}^n ω(g₁,...,g_ig_{i+1},...,g_{n+1})^{(-1)^i} · ω(g₁,...,g_n)^{(-1)^{n+1}}

Beyond group cohomology (Freed-Hopkins 2016): SPT phases for fermionic systems and systems with anti-unitary symmetries are classified by generalized cohomology theories (cobordism groups). The classification is:
SPT_d(G) ≅ [BG, Σ^d I_{U(1)} MSpin]
where MSpin is the Thom spectrum and I_{U(1)} is the Anderson dual.

**6. Entanglement Entropy as Phase Invariant**

For a gapped ground state |ψ⟩, divide space into region A and complement B:
- Reduced density matrix: ρ_A = Tr_B |ψ⟩⟨ψ|
- Entanglement entropy: S(A) = -Tr(ρ_A log ρ_A)

Area law: For gapped ground states in d spatial dimensions, S(A) ≤ α · Area(∂A) (volume law is for excited states; logarithmic corrections at criticality).

Topological entanglement entropy:
- S(A) = α · |∂A| − γ + O(e^{-|A|/ξ})
- γ = log D (universal topological constant, D = total quantum dimension of the UMTC)
- γ > 0 detects topological order; γ = 0 for trivially gapped phases

The Renyi entropy S_α = (1/(1-α)) log Tr(ρ_A^α) provides a family of topological invariants.

### Key Theorems and Definitions

**Theorem (Kitaev-Preskill, Levin-Wen, 2006)**: For any topologically ordered gapped ground state in 2D, the entanglement entropy of a large disk-shaped region A satisfies S(A) = α|∂A| − γ + O(1/|A|), where γ = log D and D is the total quantum dimension of the anyon theory.

**Theorem (Tannaka-Krein reconstruction, Drinfeld center)**: The UMTC of a (2+1)D topological phase is the Drinfeld center Z(C) of the fusion category C underlying the string-net model: Z(C) has objects (a, σ) where a ∈ C and σ is a half-braiding of a with all objects of C.

**Theorem (Verlinde formula)**: In a UMTC, the modular S-matrix diagonalizes the fusion rules:
N^c_{ab} = ∑_x (S_{ax} S_{bx} S̄_{cx}) / S_{0x}
where 0 = vacuum sector and S̄ denotes complex conjugate.

**Definition (Anyon)**: A quasi-particle excitation in a (2+1)D topological phase characterized by topological spin θ_a = e^{2πi h_a} and braiding statistics (R-matrix). Abelian anyons: all R^{ab}_c are phases. Non-abelian anyons: the R-matrix acts nontrivially on a degenerate fusion space (protected quantum memory).

**Definition (Long-range entangled vs. short-range entangled)**: A gapped ground state is long-range entangled (LRE) if it cannot be transformed into a product state by a finite-depth local quantum circuit. LRE = topological order (with anyons); SRE = either trivial phase or SPT phase.

## Law III — Frequency-modulated Processes

### Scope
Law III extends the modular framework temporally. If Law II classifies equilibrium phases as functorial classes over symmetry categories, Law III classifies non-equilibrium periodic phases as functorial classes over the time-translation category with a period-T structure. The mathematical structure is Floquet theory: the Schrödinger equation with a T-periodic Hamiltonian H(t+T) = H(t) has solutions related by a unitary evolution operator U(T) = T exp(-i∫₀ᵀ H(t) dt). The eigenvalues e^{-i ε_α T} of U(T) define the quasi-energies ε_α ∈ (-π/T, π/T] (the Floquet Brillouin zone). The central claim is that Floquet evolution is a monoidal functor from a periodized time category to the category of quantum channels, and that new phases (Floquet phases, time crystals) are characterized by natural transformations on these functors that have no equilibrium analog.

### Key Concepts

**1. Floquet Theory: Mathematical Setup**

For a T-periodic Hamiltonian H(t) = H(t+T), the Floquet theorem states that the time-evolution operator factors as:
```
U(t,0) = P(t) · e^{-i H_F t}
```
where P(t) is T-periodic (the micromotion operator) and H_F is the time-independent *Floquet Hamiltonian*.

The stroboscopic evolution U(T) = e^{-i H_F T} is a unitary operator whose spectrum (quasi-energy spectrum) encodes the long-time stroboscopic behavior.

The quasi-energy spectrum lives in the Floquet Brillouin zone (-π/T, π/T], which is the Pontryagin dual of Z (stroboscopic time steps). This is the temporal analog of crystal momentum in a spatial crystal.

**Categorical interpretation**: Let T_T be the category with objects Z (discrete time steps) and morphisms n → n+k for k ≥ 0 (forward time evolution), tensored by Z_T (period group). A Floquet system is a monoidal functor:
```
F: (Z_T-Graded) → QChan
```
where QChan is the category of quantum channels (CPTP maps) on the system Hilbert space. The Floquet Hamiltonian H_F is (up to gauge) the logarithm of F applied to one period.

**2. Magnus Expansion and Effective Hamiltonians**

For drive frequency ω = 2π/T ≫ J (local energy scales), the Floquet Hamiltonian H_F is well-approximated by the Magnus/Floquet-Magnus (FM) expansion:

H_F = H^{(0)} + H^{(1)} + H^{(2)} + ...

where:
- H^{(0)} = (1/T)∫₀ᵀ H(t) dt (time-averaged Hamiltonian)
- H^{(1)} = (1/2iT)∫₀ᵀ dt₁ ∫₀^{t₁} dt₂ [H(t₁), H(t₂)] (first commutator correction)
- H^{(n)} = O((J/ω)^n · J) (suppressed at high frequency)

The FM expansion is an asymptotic series — it diverges for generic interacting systems at long times due to Floquet heating (the system heats to infinite temperature). The *prethermal regime*: for time 0 ≤ t ≤ τ* ~ exp(c·ω/J), the system is governed by H_F^{(N)} (finite FM truncation) with exponentially small corrections. This prethermal effective Hamiltonian can support interesting phases (prethermal phases) that look like equilibrium phases of H^{(0)}.

**3. Time Crystals**

A *discrete time crystal* (DTC) is a phase of matter that spontaneously breaks the discrete time-translation symmetry of a Floquet drive:
- The system is driven with period T
- The local order parameter oscillates with period 2T (or nT for integer n)
- This oscillation persists in the thermodynamic limit, survives weak perturbations, and does not decay — it is a genuine non-equilibrium order

Mechanism (Else-Bauer-Nayak 2016): In a many-body localized (MBL) Floquet system with a π-pulse drive, the effective Floquet Hamiltonian H_F has a Z_2 symmetry that is spontaneously broken in the ground state. The order parameter ⟨σ^z_i(nT)⟩ = (-1)^n · m oscillates at twice the drive period.

The DTC is the "temporal SSB" analog: just as a spatial crystal breaks continuous space-translation symmetry to a discrete subgroup, the DTC breaks discrete time-translation symmetry (period T) to a longer period (2T, 3T, ...).

Categorical interpretation: The DTC is a natural transformation between the trivial Floquet functor (period T evolution) and the DTC Floquet functor (effectively period 2T evolution on the ordered subspace), detecting the spontaneous Z_2 symmetry breaking.

**4. Floquet Topological Insulators**

Floquet topological insulators extend the topological band theory of Law II to periodically driven systems. Key distinction:
- Equilibrium topological insulators: classified by K-theory groups K(BZ × BG) (Kitaev's 10-fold way, Altland-Zirnbauer)
- Floquet topological insulators: classified by an extended classification that includes phases with no equilibrium analog

The *anomalous Floquet insulator* (AFI): a Floquet phase where ALL bands have zero Chern number (trivial equilibrium topology) yet chiral edge modes exist at quasi-energy 0 and π. The AFI is characterized by a Floquet winding number ν ∈ Z defined from the full quasi-energy spectrum (not just the bands individually).

Floquet-Bloch theory: For a spatially periodic Floquet system (crystal + drive), the quasi-energy spectrum ε(k, q) depends on both crystal momentum k ∈ BZ and quasi-energy index q ∈ (-π/T, π/T]. This is the product of the spatial and temporal Brillouin zones.

Classification (Roy-Harper 2017): The periodic table for Floquet topological insulators in d dimensions with symmetry class (Altland-Zirnbauer label) has K-theory groups that reduce to the equilibrium periodic table in the static limit, but with additional "anomalous" classes in each dimension.

**5. Prethermal Phases and Floquet Engineering**

Floquet engineering exploits the prethermal regime to realize novel effective Hamiltonians H_eff ≈ H_F^{(N)} with engineered interactions:
- Shaken optical lattices: driving a cold-atom lattice at frequency ω renormalizes the hopping amplitude, enabling simulation of frustrated magnets and topological phases
- Light-induced superconductivity: driving phonon modes in cuprates may transiently enhance superconducting correlations
- Dynamical decoupling: alternating drive pulses suppress unwanted couplings in quantum computers

The prethermal phase diagram: In the prethermal regime (time ≤ τ* = exp(c·ω/J)):
- Prethermal equilibrium phases: effectively governed by H^{(0)} (time-averaged Hamiltonian), all Law II phases apply
- Prethermal discrete time crystals: H^{(0)} has a spontaneously broken discrete symmetry, and the micromotion induces oscillation at period 2T
- Prethermal SPT phases: H^{(0)} is an SPT phase; the full Floquet evolution adds Floquet-specific signatures

**6. Harmonic Analysis and Natural Transformations**

The Floquet approach to a T-periodic system is deeply connected to harmonic analysis on the circle S¹ = R/TZ:
- States: Floquet modes ψ(t) = e^{-iεt} u(t), u(t+T) = u(t), encode in Fourier modes u_n = (1/T)∫₀ᵀ u(t) e^{inωt} dt
- The Floquet operator ∂_t - H(t) acts on the extended Hilbert space F = L²(S¹) ⊗ H (Sambe space)
- The quasi-energy spectrum in Sambe space: {ε_α + nω : n ∈ Z} — infinite "ladder" copies of each Floquet eigenvalue

Natural transformation interpretation: A drive protocol is a path in the space of T-periodic Hamiltonians Ham(S¹). Floquet phases are connected components of this space. Phase boundaries occur when the quasi-energy gap closes. Adiabatic variation of drive parameters traces a natural transformation between Floquet functors.

### Key Theorems and Definitions

**Theorem (Floquet, 1883)**: If H(t) is T-periodic, the evolution operator U(t,0) can be written as U(t,0) = P(t) e^{-iH_F t}, where P(t) is T-periodic and P(0) = 1. The Floquet Hamiltonian H_F is defined up to multiples of ω/2π.

**Theorem (Else-Bauer-Nayak 2016)**: In a 1D Floquet spin chain with MBL, time-reversal symmetry, and a Z_2 symmetry, there exists a phase (the DTC phase) where the stroboscopic order parameter ⟨σ^z_i(nT)⟩ oscillates with period 2T for all times, even under perturbations that preserve the drive symmetry but otherwise break all spatial symmetry.

**Theorem (Magnus, 1954)**: The solution X(t) of X'(t) = A(t)X(t) with X(0) = I can be written X(t) = exp(Ω(t)) where Ω(t) is given by the Magnus series Ω = ∑_{n=1}^∞ Ω_n, with Ω_1 = ∫₀ᵗ A(t₁)dt₁, Ω_2 = (1/2)∫₀ᵗ dt₁∫₀^{t₁} dt₂ [A(t₁),A(t₂)], etc.

**Definition (Floquet Winding Number)**: For a 2D anomalous Floquet insulator, the winding number ν ∈ Z is defined from the unitary map U(k,T): BZ → U(N) (Floquet evolution over one period as a function of crystal momentum) via:
ν = (1/24π²) ∫_{BZ×S¹} Tr[(U†dU)³]
This invariant detects the AFI phase and equals the number of chiral edge modes at quasi-energy π.

**Definition (Prethermal Phase)**: A prethermal phase of a Floquet system is a phase of the effective Hamiltonian H_eff (finite Magnus truncation) that persists for all times t ≤ τ* ~ exp(cω/J), where ω is the drive frequency and J is the local interaction scale.

**Definition (Time Crystal)**: A many-body phase with spontaneously broken time-translation symmetry, characterized by a local order parameter m(t) satisfying m(t+T) = m(t+nT/p) for some integer p > 1 (period multiplication), that is: (i) nonzero in the thermodynamic limit, (ii) robust to generic perturbations of the drive.

## Law IV — Information-bearing Structures

### Scope
Law IV is the culminating layer of the modular framework. It argues that when the structures of Laws I–III are composed — monoidal categories of quantum systems, topological phases as functorial classes, and periodic drives as temporal functors — the resulting compositional complexity is sufficient to produce emergent information geometry and, from that geometry, emergent spacetime. The central thesis is: (1) sufficiently large, entangled quantum systems are quantum error-correcting codes; (2) holographic codes (HaPPY-type) realize AdS/CFT duality as an isometric functor from bulk to boundary; (3) the Fisher information metric on state space provides a Riemannian metric whose geodesics and curvature reproduce features of general relativity; and (4) therefore, spacetime geometry is emergent from the information-theoretic structure of the compositional quantum system.

### Key Concepts

**1. Quantum Error Correction as Categorical Structure**

A quantum error-correcting code (QECC) is an isometric embedding enc: H_L → H_P (logical into physical Hilbert space) such that any error E from an error set E can be detected and corrected.

Categorical formulation: A QECC is a sub-object enc: C_L ↪ C_P in the category Hilb (or more generally, a monomorphism in a †-category). The Knill-Laflamme conditions for a QECC:
```
⟨ψ_i|E†_a E_b|ψ_j⟩ = C_{ab} δ_{ij}
```
(for logical basis states ψ_i and error operators E_a, E_b) say that errors are either detectable or correctable — they cannot coherently distinguish logical states.

In the category CP of quantum channels: a QECC defines a commutative diagram:
```
H_L → (enc) → H_P → (noise N) → H_P → (rec R) → H_L
```
where R ∘ N ∘ enc ≈ id_{H_L} (recovery is approximate for generic errors, exact for correctable ones).

*Operator algebra quantum error correction* (OAQEC, Bény-Oreshkov 2007, Harlow 2017): A code corrects an error E for a subalgebra A of logical operators if there exists a recovery channel R such that R ∘ E ∘ enc ∘ a = a ∘ R ∘ E ∘ enc for all a ∈ A. This is the correct generalization for holography, where different bulk regions correspond to different error-correctable subalgebras.

**2. Holographic Codes and AdS/CFT**

The HaPPY code (Pastawski-Yoshida-Harlow-Preskill 2015): Built from perfect tensors tiling hyperbolic space.

*Perfect tensor*: A tensor T_{i₁...i_{2n}} is perfect if for any bipartition of its 2n indices into A and B with |A| = |B| = n, the corresponding linear map T: (C^d)^⊗n → (C^d)^⊗n is an isometry (unitary). Equivalently, T is a [[2n, 0, n+1]]_d quantum error-correcting code.

The HaPPY tensor network: Place a perfect tensor (e.g., a [[6,1,4]] hexagonal tensor) at each vertex of a {5,4} pentagonal tiling of the hyperbolic disk H². Connect adjacent tensors by contracting shared indices. Leave one "dangling" index per boundary tile as the physical (boundary) index, and one "bulk" index per tensor as the logical (bulk) index.

Properties of the HaPPY code:
- Isometry: The network defines an isometric embedding V: H_bulk → H_boundary
- Ryu-Takayanagi formula: For any connected boundary region A, S(A) = |γ_A|/4G_N exactly, where γ_A is the minimum number of bulk legs cut to disconnect A from the rest of the network (= geodesic length in the hyperbolic tiling)
- Entanglement wedge reconstruction: For any connected boundary region A, any bulk operator in the entanglement wedge W(A) = {bulk region homologous to A} can be reconstructed from boundary region A — even if A is "too small" (sub-AdS), the code structure allows reconstruction
- Error correction: The code corrects erasure of any boundary region A if A is not large enough to contain a geodesic homologous to the bulk (OAQEC)

Categorical structure of holographic codes: The assignment A ↦ W(A) (boundary region to entanglement wedge) is a functor from the category of boundary intervals (with inclusions as morphisms) to the category of bulk regions (with inclusions). This functor is the "holographic dictionary" as a sheaf on the boundary.

**3. AdS/CFT Duality as a Functor**

The AdS/CFT correspondence (Maldacena 1997) asserts that quantum gravity on AdS_{d+1} is exactly dual to a CFT_d on its boundary ∂AdS_{d+1} ≅ R^{d-1,1}.

Dictionary (Gubser-Klebanov-Polyakov, Witten 1998):
- Bulk field φ with mass m ↔ boundary operator O with conformal dimension Δ = (d/2) + √((d/2)² + m²R²)
- Partition function: Z_AdS[φ_0] = Z_CFT[φ_0] where φ_0 = boundary condition for φ
- Generating functional: ⟨O(x₁)...O(x_n)⟩_CFT = δ^n log Z_AdS / δφ_0(x₁)...δφ_0(x_n)

Categorical formulation: AdS/CFT is an equivalence of categories (or at least a functor) between:
- Cat_bulk: the (∞-)category of bulk quantum gravity states (with morphisms as bulk evolution operators)
- Cat_boundary: the (∞-)category of CFT states on the boundary

The functor is not just any functor — it is an isometry for the inner product structure inherited from the Hilbert space:
```
F: Cat_bulk → Cat_boundary,  ⟨ψ|φ⟩_bulk = ⟨F(ψ)|F(φ)⟩_boundary
```

The Ryu-Takayanagi formula is a consequence of this functor applied to reduced density matrices:
- ρ_A = Tr_{B} |ψ⟩⟨ψ|_boundary
- S(A) = -Tr(ρ_A log ρ_A) = Area(γ_A) / (4G_N)

This formula makes spacetime geometry (area of a surface γ_A) a function of quantum information (entanglement entropy) — the emergent nature of geometry is built into the functor.

**4. Fisher Information Metric and Information Geometry**

The *Fisher information metric* on a statistical manifold M = {p_θ : θ ∈ Θ} of probability distributions:
```
g_{ij}(θ) = E_θ[∂_i log p_θ · ∂_j log p_θ] = -E_θ[∂_i ∂_j log p_θ]
```
is a Riemannian metric satisfying:
- Positivity: g is positive definite
- Monotonicity (Chentsov): g is the unique (up to constant) Riemannian metric that is monotone under sufficient statistics (information-decreasing maps)
- Geodesics: e-geodesics (exponential family) and m-geodesics (mixture family) are dually flat; the divergence function D_α interpolates between them

*Quantum Fisher metric*: For a parameterized family of density matrices ρ_θ, the quantum Fisher metric (quantum Cramér-Rao) is:
```
g_{ij}^Q(θ) = (1/2) Tr(ρ_θ {L_i, L_j})
```
where L_i (symmetric logarithmic derivative, SLD) satisfies ∂_i ρ_θ = (ρ_θ L_i + L_i ρ_θ)/2.

*Bures metric*: The Bures metric on density matrices ds² = (1/2) Tr(dρ L) = (1/4) g^Q_{ij} dθ^i dθ^j is a Riemannian metric on the space of quantum states.

Emergence of spacetime from information geometry: 

The key conjecture (motivated by [S17], [S19], [S20]): Consider a large quantum system with many degrees of freedom (e.g., a CFT). The space of states (modular Hamiltonian space) has a natural metric given by the relative modular operator or the quantum Fisher metric. The geometry of this metric space — its Riemannian structure, geodesics, curvature — reproduces the emergent AdS spacetime of the holographic dual.

Concretely: if we define the "distance" between two states ρ and σ as the Bures distance d_B(ρ,σ) = √(2 - 2F(ρ,σ)) where F(ρ,σ) = Tr√(√ρ σ √ρ) is the fidelity, then on the space of states of a 2D CFT, the induced metric structure on modular Hamiltonian flows reproduces the AdS_3 metric (Miyaji-Takayanagi conjecture, 2015).

**6. Information-Theoretic Spacetime: Key Results**

*Modular Hamiltonian*: For a state ρ and a region A, the modular Hamiltonian K_A = -log ρ_A generates the modular flow σ_A^t = e^{-itK_A} ρ_A e^{itK_A}. In the vacuum state of a CFT, modular flow for a half-space is Lorentz boosts (Bisognano-Wichmann theorem).

*Entanglement first law*: δS(A) = Tr(K_A δρ_A) relates first-order perturbations of entanglement entropy to expectation values of the modular Hamiltonian — this is the quantum information analog of the first law of thermodynamics.

*Linearized Einstein equations from entanglement*: Faulkner-Guica-Hartman-Myers-Van Raamsdonk (2014) showed that the first law of entanglement for all ball-shaped regions in a CFT dual to a gravitational theory implies the linearized Einstein equations in the bulk. Conversely, violation of the linearized Einstein equations would imply violations of entanglement first law.

*Relative entropy and bulk geometry*: The relative entropy S(ρ||σ) = Tr(ρ log ρ) - Tr(ρ log σ) ≥ 0 (Klein's inequality) implies, in holography, the positive energy theorem in the bulk and the Bousso bound on covariant entropy.

**Theorem (Chentsov, 1982)**: The Fisher information metric g^F is the unique (up to positive proportionality constant) Riemannian metric on the n-simplex of probability distributions that is monotone under stochastic maps (Markov maps).

**Definition (Holographic Code)**: A holographic code is a quantum error-correcting code enc: H_bulk → H_boundary constructed from a tensor network on a (d+1)-dimensional hyperbolic space, such that the code subspace satisfies the Ryu-Takayanagi formula and operator algebra quantum error correction for any boundary region A whose complement B is smaller than the "homology phase transition" threshold.

**Definition (Modular Hamiltonian)**: For a density matrix ρ_A = Tr_B |ψ⟩⟨ψ|, the modular Hamiltonian is K_A = -log ρ_A. The modular flow is the one-parameter group σ_A^t(O) = e^{itK_A} O e^{-itK_A}. ## Supplementary: Key Mathematical Structures Reference

### Braided Monoidal Categories (for anyons)

A braided monoidal category has a braiding natural isomorphism β_{A,B}: A⊗B → B⊗A satisfying the hexagon equations:
```
β_{A,B⊗C} = (id_B ⊗ β_{A,C}) ∘ (β_{A,B} ⊗ id_C)
β_{A⊗B,C} = (β_{A,C} ⊗ id_B) ∘ (id_A ⊗ β_{B,C})
```
A *ribbon category* additionally has a twist θ_A: A → A (topological spin) satisfying θ_{A⊗B} = β_{B,A} ∘ β_{A,B} ∘ (θ_A ⊗ θ_B).

A *modular tensor category* is a semisimple ribbon category over a field k with:
- Finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects {X_i}
- Non-degenerate S-matrix: S_{ij} = Tr(β_{X_j,X_i} ∘ β_{X_i,X_j})/D (S is invertible)

### Topological Quantum Field Theories (recap)

An *n-dimensional TQFT* is a symmetric monoidal functor Z: Cob_n → Vect_k where:
- Objects of Cob_n: closed oriented (n-1)-manifolds Σ
- Morphisms of Cob_n: oriented n-dimensional bordisms M: Σ → Σ' (manifolds with boundary ∂M = Σ ∐ Σ')
- Monoidal structure: disjoint union ∐

## Gaps and Research Priorities

**Critical gaps this framework must address:**

1. **Law I**: The passage from finite-dimensional categorical quantum mechanics (FHilb) to infinite-dimensional QFT requires rigorous treatment. Workers should consult the Haag-Kastler axioms (algebraic QFT) and Costello-Gwilliam factorization algebras.

2. **Law II**: Fracton topological order is not captured by any currently known MTC. A categorical framework for fractons remains an open research program — workers should note this explicitly and cite Vijay-Haah-Fu (2016) as the founding reference.

3. **Law III**: The rigorous mathematical definition of "Floquet phase" (analogous to equilibrium phases) is ongoing research. The stroboscopic equivalence class of Floquet Hamiltonians is not fully characterized; workers should treat this carefully and distinguish "Floquet phase of micromotion" from "Floquet phase of stroboscopic evolution."

4. **Law IV**: The RT formula has been proved in a semiclassical limit. A full non-perturbative quantum gravity derivation remains open. Workers should present the HaPPY code as a discrete toy model that satisfies RT exactly but does not capture all features of the continuum.

5. **Synthesis**: The claim that Laws I–IV compose to produce classical spacetime is a research program, not a theorem. Workers should frame the synthesis as a conjecture supported by the above evidence, not as an established result.

**Workers should note**: The passage from any law to the next (the "Lift" functors) is conjectural at various levels of rigor. Law I → Law II has the most mathematical backing (classification theorems are rigorous). Law II → Law III is partially rigorous (for MBL-protected Floquet phases). Law III → Law IV is largely programmatic. Presenting these as levels of a modular research agenda — with increasing speculative content at higher levels — is the recommended framing.
